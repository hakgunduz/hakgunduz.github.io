A long time ago, in a galaxy far, far, away…

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*9Q-KfBl3-ebwkrsMebNgaA.jpeg)

The first thing that struck me when reading the screenplay is that how well-written the screenplay is. Every beat is there for a reason. And Lawrence Kasdan knows his characters really well. The story and the screenplay is top notch start to finish. I think, that’s why it’s most people’s favorite Star Wars movie. This episode has visceral effect on us.

Opening crawl explains the main drive of the movie. Darth Vader. He is looking for Luke Skywalker and has dispatched thousands of remote probes into the far reaches of space.

Luke and the rest of the rebels are hiding on the planet called Hoth. One of Vader’s probes crashes down to the planet. Where Luke finished his circle. He wants to take a look at the meteorite site but — an ice creature shows up attacking him.

At the rebel base, Han and Chewie is working on the Millenium Falcon. It needs fixing. And Han needs to leave there’s a bounty on his head.

Leia doesn’t want him to leave but can’t say it. Only says that rebels need a good leader. Han sees through her and gets angry because she doesn’t just say she wants him to stay. There’s a conflict between them. And affection at the same time.

But Han turns back when he hears that Luke is missing. He rides out the Tauntaun to search for his friend.

Luke kills the ice creature and gets out to snowdrift. And sees Ben Kenobi there. He says Luke needs to go to Dagobah system to find master Yoda and learn from him. Then, Han finds. They return to the base.

The imperial probe finds them. Darth Vader’s Star Destroyer is there. Rebels start the evacuation. I think this is the main inciting incident in the movie. But there are lots of thing going on till that point and throughout. Script tangles them up and connects them.

Ice Creature attack leads Luke to Ben Kenobi and the revelation of his object. And at the same time, because of that attack, Luke failed to stop the Imperial probe. And it found the Rebel Base. And other sub-plots; rebel fleet, Han and Leia, Millenium Falcon.

And in this point, they all go to different way. Luke goes to Dagobah System, Rebel fleet jumps into hyperspace, Millenium Falcon goes into the asteroids. And they all want to get back together starting this point. Their **tranquil existence** demolished by the Imperial fleet and they went into **unknown**.

One of the other aspects of this script is this — everything goes awfully wrong all the time.

Rebels prepare for combat but speeders are down one by one by the Imperial walkers.

Meanwhile Han tries to fix the Millenium Falcon. Finds Leia in the midst of this and takes her to the Falcon. When they’re getting ready to escape. And almost all the other time. Nothing stops on the background. Stormtroopers get ready too. They set the canons. Cool way to not lose the tension in the scene.

Then, it’s Millenium Falcon v. the Star Destroyers. Han wants to jump into hyperspace right away but it doesn’t work. Pay off to the repair scenes. They didn’t have time to fix it.

Something hits them. Not a laser, not the TIE fighters. It’s an asteroid. And Han immediately changes the plan and heads into the asteroids. Others resist. Because it’s madness. But not for Han. Instead of just jump into hyperspace. It doesn’t work. Remember, everything always goes wrong. But Han seizes his chance. They can’t follow them in the asteroids. And he moves closer to the big ones. Falcon moves inside the giant asteroid crater.

Luke arrives to the Dagobah. Not just lands. Place is in fog — nothing can be seen. System failures. And his X-wing crashes down to the swamp. ARTOO falls off. Something tries to eat it.

Vader’s scars. Reveal. The script doesn’t try to over explain this. Just tells what we see. Plain and simple. And effective.

In the crater, they try to fix the hyperdrive. Han-Leia conflict continues.

Luke meets master Yoda. And reading this scene, a revelation came to me. Yoda is the way he is (bluish and small-in the screenplay) Because of the trait that script carries. Everything must go wrong. Luke expects a great warrior and WANTS to learn to be a great warrior from him. But instead finds this **little creature**. But you know, it’s master Yoda. He has wisdom. Which Luke NEEDS.

None of the scenes plays without an any conflict. Almost every character is in conflict with other characters. Tug-o-war over the lamp between ARTOO and Master Yoda.

And around page 60. Han kisses Leia. Their love-plot continues.

Emperor talks to the Vader and informs him about the disturbance in the force. Luke must not be a Jedi. But Vader talks about turning Luke to the dark side. Soft spot for Luke. Setup for one of the biggest revelation in the movie history.

No patience in Luke. Much anger in him like his father. Yoda doesn’t want to train Luke. Tells him about why he’s not ready. Luke tries to convince him. Again, it could be a regular training sequence but instead Yoda hesitates.

In the crater, something is attacking the Falcon. In fact, that’s no cave. It’s a giant space slug.

Luke goes into the tree cave. The dark side. And sees Darth Vader there. Quickly decapitates him and sees his own face in the mask.

Emperor’s warning made Vader hire bounty hunters. Just then, Falcon comes out of the crater but hyperdrive doesn’t work. Again. And the shield on the back quarter is down. Han turns the Falcon head on to the Imperial Star Destroyer and hides in the plain sight.

Meanwhile Luke’s training continues. Luke resists. Not believing. Yoda shows him the way. He starts to believe.

Vader alerts all the commanders and starts searching them in the entire galaxy. But Falcon is clinging to the side of one of the Star Destroyers.

Leia shuts off THREEPIO and asks Han’s next move. She’s changing her mind about him. New plan, they go to Lando Calrissian. But one bounty hunter, Boba Fett is following them.

Just when Luke starts to learn to control the force, he sees his friends — Han and Leia in distress.

Millenium Falcon arrives in the Bespin. They meet Lando there. And Lando leads them straight to Darth Vader. It’s a trap.

Luke leaves before completing the training. Ben Kenobi and Master Yoda try to convince him otherwise. Luke leaves anyway.

Vader uses them as bait for Luke. And he’s coming into the trap.

Lando tries to help but he has his own reasons. Every character has desires, and reasons in the screenplay. No one is flat. They’re all multi-dimensional like real life.

They put Han in carbon freezer and Boba Fett takes him to Jabba the Hut. The bounty on Han’s head. Pay-off.

Right then, Lando’s men takes out the stormtroopers. They go after Boba Fett to save Han but they can’t catch Fett’s ship.

And Vader confronts Luke. First Luke charges on him but quickly fails. And comes back — more calm and defensive as Yoda taught. But Vader is strong. He tests Luke.

Meantime — Leia, Chewie, and Lando escape with the Millenium Falcon.

Vader cuts Luke’s hand. Not just kills him. Tries to take him to the dark side. Luke resists. And right then, huge reveal drop — Vader is Luke’s father. He’s Anakin Skywalker.

Everything changes for Luke after learning this. Vader was a bad guy who he wants to destroy but he learned that Vader is his father.

Then, a calm comes over Luke and makes a decision. He steps off the gantry platform into space. Falls to the exhaust pipe.

Then, Luke force-calls Leia. She turns back for him. Meanwhile, three TIE fighters on their tail.

Vader is going to his Star Destroyer too.

They take Luke and try to go into hyperspace but IT DOESN’T WORK AGAIN.

BRUTAL — BRUTAL KASDAN IS.

Vader’s men deactivated the hyperdrive and his Star Destroyer coming closer — actives the tractor beam.

ARTOO fixes the hyperdrive and Falcon unexpectedly jumps into hyperspace.

And they’re back with the rest of the fleet. Back to known. Back to their tranquil existence. Only Han is not with them but Lando and Chewie leaves to find him.

The screenplay ends with Luke and his sister Leia looking out to space.

I always wondered how this ending worked. It’s a dark note but I felt hope. And uplifted. They’re defeated but I didn’t felt that. And then I noticed, it’s always been about Emperor v. Rebels. I saw the actual inciting incident in the story. Imperial fleet’s arrival to the Hoth planet. Rebels forced to leave.

They were together. Imperial fleet scattered them but now they’re back together. More strong.
