First off, no slug line. This script is insane.

But, you can still follow where and when the actions taking place. It even adds some sort of flow to the script. I think, it’s perfect for tying up the scenes together. You cannot jump from scene to another this way. You supposed to structure it beforehand.

You can write and follow the flow on your screenplay this way but at the end I recommend putting the slug lines on. Dan Gilroy didn’t need to because he wrote and directed this movie.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*oQ69NSeE1v-AWcj7fhDk0g.jpeg)

One of the other things I notice while reading it is how much Dan Gilroy knows the subject. Nightcrawler life, news crew, police codes, etc. He did his research. It feels godly authentic.

He knows his characters in every way. Especially the protagonist, LOU. He is one hell of a piece.

LOU is an antihero. The script knows it since the beginning but it doesn’t make any difference. He doesn’t just go like kil kill kill because he’s an antihero. Situations and people push him to make decisions. He chooses the way he wants to act but the antagonism is there to confront him. Sometimes as situations, sometimes as competitors, sometimes as friend/colleague(NINA). And those antagonistic forces push LOU to act.

But **the way he acts** changes it all. And I think the power of this script and movie comes from that. The character. So powerful.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*kVk5z82W_zLOE2vOrhdaMQ.jpeg)

About changes: Not much change in the screenplay compared to the script. One scene with a woman from sex hook-up website didn’t make it to the final cut. But others mostly did. There are little bit of changes here and there but nothing major.

I noticed little nuances while reading which I didn’t while watching. LOU mentions that he is self-thought and says people can learn everything they want from the internet. This isn’t exactly a set-up and pay off situation. It gives the godly authenticity and characteristics to LOU and the script. LOU later uses what he learned from internet like managing skills, dealing with workers by dealing with RICK and managing him in the toughest situations. And he uses these information as literal as possible. Maybe even quoting them. Noticing this was such a treat. It puts a smile on my face, I don’t know why. It’s so small but makes so much difference. It’s like a sneak peak of what Dan Gilroy thought and used while writing this piece.

The **empathy** builder factors toward antihero. You’re going to need those while putting a character like this on paper. LOU speaks kindly all the time. Waters the plants (takes care of others). Hard-worker. An outsider. You connect with him in first ten pages.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*Ue5il8xeD9YXWPerx_Bj3g.jpeg)

**Screenplay’s POV is LOU’S POV.**   
It only focuses on LOU. **There’s no single scene without him.** And we constantly see **WHY** he’s doing the bad things while he’s doing it. It builds empathy too. We connect to him whatever he does. This makes the story one hell of a ride.

Just read it. Read it in one breath.

**NIGHTCRAWLER** Screenplay by Dan Gilroy — **For educational and research purposes only.**
