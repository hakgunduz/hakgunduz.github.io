When you start reading the screenplay first thing you see is how much the script changed.

First off, there is no interrogation scene.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*pBmMnqwa8_EBaS0nHTE8xA.png)

Alex Garland’s ANNIHILATION (v.10) screenplay opens with a meteor falling to Earth. Then, cuts to LENA giving a lesson: Life-cycle of cell. You immediately combine the two; first the meteor then the cell. Strong images to open a movie but —

Movie opens with the interrogation scene, the aftermath, the human experience. We first meet LENA, our protagonist and connect with her right away. It’s more intimate and more human. And we face the conflict right off. Other version, the meteor opening is some kind of a conflict too but we saw it hundred times, we got used to it. There’s nothing moving in that image. Nothing human too. Meteor opening makes our protagonist reactive while the other version, the interrogation connects all things to LENA and her decisions.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*w1TOatgM3hISYqaQ8Jmo9g.jpeg)

**It’s not a story about meteors, it’s her story.**

While reading the screenplay, you see changes similar to the opening. KANE returning home, he reveals less in the movie than in the script. He doesn’t speak much. And the SPEC OPS, there are handful of masculine men in the screenplay they have considerable amount of screen time, like they’re in the SHIMMER fighting each other.

That didn’t make it to the final cut. KANE shooting his own men, SPEC OPS fighting each other. We don’t see these on the movie. They left those details to our imagination. Did they fight each other or did the shimmer somehow swallow them? Something to talk about.

Then, the SOUTHERN REACH scenes, in screenplay LENA is a prisoner. Later she somehow breaks free and sees the shimmer. That scene on the screenplay is written to be shocking. Compared to the movie version, LENA staring in deep thoughts at the shimmer at night, the script version is weaker I think.

LENA and KANE’S six months agreement, shower scene in the SOUTHERN REACH — SPEC OPS soldier waiting on the door, elevator scene and lots of “show off scenes” like these didn’t see the final cut.

In the screenplay, VENTRESS asks LENA not to say her relation to KANE to the group. In the movie, LENA chooses not to tell. Again, it brings things down to **her story, her decisions** and it gives some kind of suspense to the journey.

“Shimmer grows and KANE doesn’t survive the move.” no need to say this, audience already connected the dots. Skill-set dialogue, the group short hands a biologist, no need to say this. Firing range scene, pretentious.

Why are they all women? THORENSEN explains it over the theories at hand. Too much revelation. Besides, you don’t have to explain that.

LENA is not an ex-soldier in the screenplay. They used to go hunting with KANE. In the movie she can take care of herself.

Tons of changes like these.

In the screenplay, we constantly see provoking images one after another with revealing dialogue on them. It gives you more but doesn’t make you care for the character or her journey. **Movie gives you less scenes like that but makes you feel so much more. It makes you feel for the character and her story.** It’s subtle, deep and moving. Things changed from huge exploding sci-fi to her psychological journey. Everything boils down to LENA’S decisions. And it’s better this way.

And dialogues are on the nose in the screenplay. They cut almost half of it. Script is more chatty and revealing, movie is discreet.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*8i56y90Ni351esucv2nyzg.jpeg)

I recommend reading Alex Garland’s screenplay. It gives you a chance to see good rewriting and how it is supposed to be done. It’s solid.
