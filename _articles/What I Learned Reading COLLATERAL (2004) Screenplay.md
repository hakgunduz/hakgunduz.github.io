It opens with Tom Cruise’s character, Vincent. With the briefcase scene. You first see the cliche, the convention of the briefcases. Someone bumps into another and turns out they got the same briefcase and they mix up.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*s_TJS-ZZO8lseMSyXUBWDQ.jpeg)

In this case, Tom Cruise bumps up to Jason Statham. Yes, the man himself, playing a small part in this movie. They got it mixed up. Vincent takes his briefcase but then you immediately realize, it was a deliberate scene… Tells about the world we’re in and the character, Vincent. He’s not just an ordinary guy.

Then, Max, Jamie Foxx’s character. The protagonist. A lot going on in the cab station when they introduce him in the screenplay. Half of it didn’t make it to final cut. No Chinese gang and the chop suey on Max’s cab.

The girl, Annie — Max doesn’t see her at first in the screenplay. In the movie, Max sees her first and backs up to show himself to her and get her to his cab. Dialogue between them — she’s an attorney. Max is a good cab driver. Tells her about his dream, limo company. Things going well. They get along well. She likes him. Then, she gets off, leaving her card behind her. Max is happy. But —

Vincent comes into his cab — and the story starts. They start chatting. Vincent doesn’t like LA. **Tells about this guy. He gets on the MTA there, dies. Six hours he’s riding the subway before anybody notices.**

Vincent has a peculiar POV of the world — the one that you can relate. He’s the harsh truth. Max says being the cab driver is part-time — then Vincent asks how long has he been a cab driver. Max answers, twelve years. It’s not part time… We start learning about Max’s character. He’s a kind of guy who dreams but never acts.

Vincent convinces Max to stick with him till the end of the night. He has appointments to make… But — he throws a dead man on to his cab… Vincent is a contract killer. And he’s there on an assignment. Takes Max as hostage. They put the body in to the trunk. And move along to the next target.

Next scene, Ray Fanning comes — Mark Ruffalo’s character. He’s a detective.

In Max’s cab, they’re arguing about the dead guy. Vincent talks about killing the guy like it’s nothing. S**ix billion people on the planet and Max gets bent out of shape ’cause of one fat guy? Tens of thousands killed before sundown at Rwanda. Nobody’s killed people that fast since Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Did he bat an eye? Did he join Amnesty International, Oxfam or something? No. But Vincent off one Angeleno, and Max throw a hissy fit…**

> **Adapt, Darwin. Shit happens. I Ching… whatever. Roll with it.**

They arrive the Target #2. Before Vincent is getting out the cab. Lenny calls, asking about the smashed glass. Says he’s paying… But Vincent doesn’t accept, starts mentoring Max.

Vincent makes him what he NEEDS to do by POINTING A GUN AT HIM. Then, he covers Max’s ass by acting as if he’s an attorney. This bit is interesting in the story — Vincent is the antagonist of the story but then again, he literally mentors Max. Criticizes him. Gives him the harsh truths. Makes him act on it. They start bonding…

And it makes the character Vincent multi-dimensional. He’s not just a bad guy. We relate to him in the story. We root for him too. We root for both of them.

He ties Max to the wheel and leaves. Max asks for help while sitting at the cab. Young white guys come and ask his money. They take Max’s wallet and Vincent’s briefcase. Then Vincent comes back —

> Yo homie… That my briefcase?

Vincent draws his gun fast. Double tap on the chest and one on the head. He’s fast and he’s pro. And he saved Max’s wallet.

New news! Vincent wants to buy Max a drink. They go to a jazz club.

Fanning on the crime scene. He senses something is going on…

On the scene, Jazz is off melody, like they’re in the night… It’s thematically connected to the plot but represented as “time to kill” for a moment. But it’s a reversal — it is the plot. Vincent went there to kill the jazz guy, the owner. He’s target #3.

In the script it’s Louis Armstrong. In the movie it’s Miles Davis they’re talking about. Max asks for a little jazz, asks Vincent to give a chance to the guy, he’s learning. Vincent asks a jazz question. The guy answers correct but he kills the guy anyway.

Then, Max’s mother calls — Vincent makes him go to his mother, makes him buy flowers. Max resists. He doesn’t want to take him to his mother.

> Since when was any of this negotiable?

Hospital scene with Max’s mother, he lied to her about the limo company. She doesn’t know he’s a cab driver. **Vincent introduces himself as his friend, a mentor.**

Finally, after Vincent talked to his mother, Max does something on his own… He takes the briefcase, and hurls it to the freeway.

Meantime, Fanning learns that someone is out there. Someone professional. Double tabs in the sternum and the one in the head saying he’s a pro. A contract killer. And he doesn’t think Feds know about it. As he’s learning about the details we get to learn about Vincent’s assignment. Why is he killing the guys?

Next dialogue between Vincent and Max, parents died when Vincent’s a kid. Reveal about his past. We relate to him. Empathy factor kicks in.

They took out the line that Max is saying he’s paying his mother's hospital bills since high school. Makes sense because then, Max becomes someone else. He’s not responsible for his failure. But taking out the line, he’s responsible. He needs to be responsible.

Next scene, Max needs to go to Felix and ask for the flash drive because he threw Vincent’s briefcase to the freeway. First, Max can’t speak or act on the level of Vincent. Felix, Javier Bardem, dominates the conversation but Max turns around the conversation using Vincent’s lines. He starts to change… He learned to speak up… And he gets out in one piece, and with the flash drive. Meeting with Felix had affect on Max.

Meanwhile, Fanning and the Feds talk about all this. They see Max from the cameras at the Felix’s place.

Next target — a club called Fever. Fanning, Feds and Felix’s men know they’re going there. Things start to heat up… And on the way, Vincent says Max should call her. The lady friend. Life is short. Given the circumstances…

At the Fever, the way Vincent takes down the bodyguards in the crowd. It’s just cold. And well-written.

> VINCENT SPOTS  
> an outer perimeter of security. Bodyguards within the crowd,  
> facing outward from the booth…looking for trouble before it  
> gets to the row of booths and Peter Yip.  
> MAX  
> receives the glance from Vincent. He’s gestured down the middle  
> of the dance floor towards the rear. They work their way  
> through the mass of bodies. Meanwhile, Vincent casually smiles  
> at a girl and takes an oblique path, sliding along a convex side  
> wall. Vincent’s path brinbrings Vincent up behind Bodyguard #1, who  
> looks to the right as…  
> BODYGUARD #1  
> pounded in the kidney. His head is twisted around and torn  
> back, dropping him to the floor amid the bodies and noise.  
> Vincent’s focus is already on BODYGUARD #2, as he kicks down  
> with ferocious force, slamming Bodyguard #1 into  
> unconsciousness…Bodyguard #2 intuits, turns. Vincent’s foot slams his knee  
> sideways, breaking it, Vincent’s palm bounces the man’s forehead  
> back, his fist slams into his exposed neck, that fast.  
> MAX  
> buffeted by dancers, saw, backs away, as…  
> BODYGUARD #3 *  
> saw the assault on #2. He grabs for Vincent…who breaks the  
> grab, pulls Bodyguard #3’s head and neck down towards him, slams  
> his knee into his rib cage twice, breaking things, spins the man  
> backwards, rips his head sideways and back, a centimeter from  
> breaking, and holding it there with his left arm, Vincent’s  
> right hand is filled with the H+K as he moves towards Peter Yip.

… the next target.

Hell break loose there. Vincent starts killing the bodyguards.

Fanning finds Max in the midst of this. He saves Max from there. He believes him.

Meanwhile — Vincent kills the target.

Fanning takes Max out to back alley. Max’s nightmare is over. Starts crying, then — BOOM. BOOM. BOOM. Fanning is dead before he hits the ground.

Vincent grabs Max. Nightmare is not over! Max is back in his front seat, driving…

Max asks why’d he have to kill Fanning? He believed him! They start arguing again.

> Get with it. Get over it.  
> …millions of galaxies of hundreds of millions of stars and a speck on one in a blink…that’s us. Lost in space. The universe don’t care (about you).  
> (beat)  
> The cop, you, me? Who notices?

**Max starts to get his fate on his own hands. Starts to accelerate. He didn’t straighten up and look at it to himself until that moment. He wanted everything to be perfect but now he sees the real truth thanks to Vincent. They’re all a speck of dust anyway. So, what difference does it make? He crashes the cab…**

Police siren comes — Vincent is gone. Did Max survive the night?

But the Police Officer sees the dead man in the trunk. Max doesn’t resist, he’s okay with getting arrested. But —

He sees from his computer that the last target is Annie, the attorney girl. His lady friend. He takes Vincent’s gun from there and points it to the Officer. The Police Officer resists… But Max hits him with Vincent’s line.

> When did this become a negotiation?

Lady in distress. And now Max is ready to save him. He’s changed. Thanks to Vincent. That’s what makes the character Vincent so interesting. He’s prepared his own doom by mentoring Max. And at the same time he’s Max’s doom. He’s the antagonist in Max’s story…

Max calls her on the way, warns her but she doesn’t have anywhere to go. Vincent is already there. He finds her but Max is also there. He shots Vincent through his ear. (Ear in the script, cheek in the movie.)

And he escapes with her. Vincent gets up immediately and goes after them. They go into the subway. The chase scene. Vincent pushes them to the first car until they don’t have anywhere to go…

Then, they face each other. The train goes dark. A BLIZZARD OF MUZLLE FLASHES. They fire through the windows at each other — glass shattering between cars.

The lights open — Max looks at his gun, empty. Vincent is still there standing… He ejects the empty magazine. Before it even hits the floor or at his feet…Vincent’s hand loading a full mag, the weapon pointed at Max. But the .45 slips from his fingers… Then, he sits. He’ s shot…

Smiles faintly.

> Hey Max. Guy. Gets on a subway. Dies. Think anybody’ll notice?
