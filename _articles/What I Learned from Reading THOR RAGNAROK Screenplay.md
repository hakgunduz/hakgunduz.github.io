“*It’s THOR. His beard is long and his clothes are worn. That rough, grizzled look of a man who’s spent years on the road. He awakens with a JOLT.”*

This is one of the first lines of the screenplay. You can see how similar it is to the comic book language. It clearly follows the comic book style and it’s consistence throughout.

I was watching Scott Frank’s interview who wrote Out of Sight, Minority Report, Godless and many other movies. He said that one of the most important aspects new screenwriters lack is consistency. Their tone and language is not consistent throughout their script. And I noted that right away. After reading Thor Ragnarok’s script I understood what he mean by that.

**WHAT’S THE WORST THING THAT CAN HAPPEN?**THOR escapes from the FIRE DRAGON but HEIMDALL is not there to open the Bi-Frost. THOR returns home but his father, ODIN is not there. He finds ODIN but learns his father LIED to him all along. Then his father, ODIN DIES. THOR’S EVIL SISTER — who is a lot stronger than he is — returns and SHATTERS THOR’S HAMMER!

Script follows this worst case scenarios and introduces a new villain to us who is a lot stronger than our protagonist. She pushes our protagonist, THOR to his limits. He loses his father, he loses his planet, he loses his people, he loses his eye — but he grows. He learns. And in the end, he out-smarts HELA with a twist. He uses Asgard’s doom, RAGNAROK to save his people.

**Plot** is so simple, clear but yet it surprises anyway. It being simple allows characters, scenes, action to thrive. It feels like as if they locked the plot first and rewrote the details million times. And found some unique gems, characters traits, actions, subplots.

And the screenplay dexterously hides the setups for the twist at the end. You read and watch the first scene just the fun of it because it’s fun. THOR makes fun of SURTUR. Then he wants to escape through the Bi-Frost but HEIMDALL is not there. You meet with this new character SKURGE and he’s fun. You watch it. These are all setups for the twist at the end but you miss it. Script is all about moments like these. It’s fun and irrelevant then it’s relevant than you realize it was a setup for something else than you see it’s a twist.

The scenes, the beats doesn’t only have one function. They all are multi-dimensional. Like the characters. Most of the characters in this screenplay are cool. And **they feel real even the most ridiculous ones.**

*THOR — You must be Hela. I’m Thor, son of Odin.  
HELA — Really, you don’t look like him.  
LOKI — Perhaps we can reach an arrangement.  
HELA — You sound like him.*

This dialogue. It made me think. You can write these kind of dialogue only if you know your characters so well. And we know THOR and LOKI after years and years. It’s simple. It’s obvious. Yet it’s effective. It made me wonder, we can write these kind of dialogue if we know our characters this much. And if you know your characters this much, you can be a lot creative and effective with your actions and dialogues.

I recommend reading this screenplay. It’s so much fun.
