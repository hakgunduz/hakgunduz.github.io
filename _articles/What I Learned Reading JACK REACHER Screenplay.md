I’ve been trying to get my hands on one of *Christopher McQuarrie*’s screenplays, especially the *Mission Impossible Rogue Nation* or *Fallout* but I couldn’t find them. What I could find is a screenplay called *One Shot*, the original title of the movie *Jack Reacher*.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1242/1*4i78lOwVK2CJ1R1XRFI4NA.jpeg)

It’s based on the novel by *Lee Child* with the same name, *One Shot*. Previous drafts by *Josh Olson*.

**Story**

It’s an action flick, a Tom Cruise movie right? I think there’s more to it. Let’s look at the premise. Murder happens, Jack Reacher — a detective — comes and solves the case. We see this story millions of times. But no. The story is not about that. It goes like this:

Murder happens, someone frames James Barr for it. Barr asks for Reacher because they have a past. Reacher comes to make sure he’s not getting away this time. He thinks Barr is guilty. But Helen, the defense attorney gives him enough suspicion to look for the evidence. Reacher starts looking at the crime scene and evidence. And he senses something is not right. And then he solves the case in his own way.

All the tweaks on the story are there to make Jack Reacher character who he is. It’s more like a character study. *That’s why the movie is called Jack Reacher not One Shot.*

**Changes**

Chris McQuarrie and Tom Cruise is famous for not giving the company a script while shooting Mission Impossible movies. It’s madness. Million dollars are on the line and you go blind in the process. But they make it work every time. And in this section, I want to track the changes in the movie to get in Mcquarrie’s and Cruise’s head. **Why they change what they change while shooting a movie?**

Emerson does all the talking with Barr. In the screenplay, half of the lines are belong to Rodin. And in the same scene Barr says “You got the wrong guy.” In the movie, he doesn’t talk. He just writes “Get Jack Reacher”. — *we know that they got the wrong guy. If Barr says that too, it weakens the impact on the scene.*

The first scene where we see Reacher, he’s with a lady friend watching TV. The lady keeps on talking while Reacher sees the name Barr on the news. Scene continues for almost two pages but in the movie it runs only in couple of seconds. Reacher sees the name Barr on the news while his lady friend is getting dressed. — *And that’s it. We don’t need anything else.*

There’s a scene where Barr is with other prisoners and they beat the shit out of him. In the movie it’s only a flashback and runs for a couple of seconds. We see the prisoners beat him. — *that’s it.*

Reacher doesn’t kiss the woman at the bus in the movie. What the fuck? Why is he kissing her in the script? Reacher doesn’t grab Attorney Rodin’s pinkie when they first meet. — *I think these scenes are there to make Reacher a more brutal person but it weakens the empathy towards him. So they cut the scenes.*

When Helen goes to talk with the victims’ family the guy just opens to door and Helen gets in. And he’s drunk in the script. They talk over the door for a while in the movie, and then he lets her in. No sign of booze in the movie. *Maybe this is because of the rating. But the scene is better in the movie.*

There is no dog in the movie. Police kill Barr’s dog in the screenplay.

Bar fight, the last two guys doesn’t run in the movie. Because they’re ‘hired’. — *It’s almost as if they didn’t think it through in the first place but when they’re shooting it someone (probably Cruise) went like “Wait a minute. If this is an actual bar fight they would run away but it’s not. So, we have to shoot it this way. All of them have to attack on Reacher.*

The car chase doesn’t end with the bus scene in the screenplay. Reacher leaves the car and runs on foot. Because he’s better at it. Police sends K9’s after him but Reacher beats the dogs. — *Again, this weakens the empathy towards Reacher. Okay, he says he’s not a hero but he’s our protagonist after all. Nobody likes a person who beats up dogs.*

In the last sequence, Martin Cash — the old guy who owns the gun range — doesn’t want to kill. In the movie the problem is the lights. They can’t see the shooter. And Cash says he’s not going to start until the shooter on the other side starts. And he suggests Reacher to start running.

There’s no fight scene between Charlie, the shooter and Reacher in the screenplay. Reacher just shoots him several times before killing him. In the movie, they fight. *Reacher makes it personal, and he knows he can take out the guy in fare fight. So, he just goes with it.* *The scene has more impact on the audience this way. In the script Charlie asks Reacher to kill him directly. But Reacher shoots him on his foot instead. We would’ve feel sorry for Charlie that way. But in the movie, he feels good about himself when Reacher drops the rifle. So, we root for Reacher.*

Helen kisses Reacher at the end of the screenplay. But not in the movie. *It makes the story a cheesy one. No need to complicate things. He helped her and that’s it.*

**Clarity**

The plot of this screenplay is very simple. Someone frames Barr for the murders, Reacher gets involved and solves the case. And goes after the actual bad guys.

The choice of showing the actual shooter in the first scene gives the audience clarity. They know, Barr is framed for the murders. That creates anticipation. And when Reacher gets involved. We expect certain things from him because he’s the protagonist. And he doesn’t disappoint. He quickly solves the case and even gets ahead of us. We don’t know why the actual bad guys did what they did, we learn it from Reacher when he solves the case. It leaves impact on us. We’re impressed.

**If we track the beats of the plot,**

First ten pages end with Emerson asking “Who the hell is Jack Reacher?”

Reacher comes and talks with Emerson and Rodin. Then, he meets Helen. And the first twenty pages end with Reacher saying he’s there to bury Barr.

In the next ten pages, Helen and Reacher try to convince each other with Barr being guilty or not. First thirty pages end with Reacher coming back from the bus. He’s going to look at the crime scene and the evidence.

In the next ten pages, he starts looking at the scene and the evidence while Helen is talking with the victims’ family.

In the next ten pages, bar fight happens starting on page forty. Reacher senses something is up.

At the page fifty, Helen starts telling stories of the victims.

At the page sixty, Reacher goes to find Sandy. She is the girl from the bar. Reacher takes her car and goes to the guy’s house. Someone attacks him again at the house. Reacher’s suspicions are growing by this point.

In the next ten pages, page seventy to eighty — Reacher talks with Helen. On page seventy-five, he says James Barr is innocent. And on the page eighty he explains the real reason. Oline Archer.

On page eighty-one, the shooter kills Sandy and tries to frame Reacher for it. Police chase Reacher but he gets away.

On page ninety, Emerson and Rodin are at Helen’s house. Reacher is guilty. But the phone rings. Reacher talks with Helen and explains what’s happening. Then, he goes to gun range where he meets the owner Martin Cash and —

— on page hundred, he finds the shooter.

But right after that, they kidnap Helen…

The last sequence is Reacher coming to save Helen while killing the bad guys.

It’s simple and well structured. Everything is there to make Reacher, Reacher. But in the script, we’re not always rooting for him. They saw this and changed it with couple of little tweaks. Everything boils down to where he says he’s not a hero. He’s a drifter. He has nothing to lose. But we root for him. He’s on our team. He’s our drifter…

Overall, I think McQuarrie and Cruise’s biggest concerns from any story are two things: Is the plot clear and easy to follow? Is the protagonist someone who the audience would root for?

## Character and the story come first, spectacle comes after.

This applies for Mission Impossible movies also. Tom Cruise and Christopher McQuarrie know this. That’s why they’re good storytellers.
