First of all, this is one of the most iconic movies. And the screenplay is the holy bible of screenwriting. Every once in a while I return to it and read again.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1128/1*Kn7m1Ss0lFkbgVImE0SGaw.jpeg)

**1. Minimalist, haiku-like style, and white space.**

I learned that you can tell a story with using a few simple words and lots of white space. The script is using the words economically and effectively. It doesn’t over describe, it just follows the story. Every beat is there for a reason, and with a few words, it paints a picture in the reader’s mind.

**2. The creature.**

The screenplay never over describes the creature. It tells its features, it tells what it does beat by beat. It never fully describes it just the fun of it. When it kills Brett, the screenplay only says the fact that it’s bigger than before. Which is crucial for the story. Because Ripley’s plan of catching it with a net is off the table after they see the creature is bigger.

**3. Structure.**

First 10 pages; introduction of the characters, and the first conflict — they’re out of their system. Dallas talks to the Mother and learns about the transmission coming from the planet close to them.

Page 10. They go into the planet where the transmission coming from. The ship shuts down, they’re trapped.

Page 20. They step out from the ship to the planet’s surface to go where the transmission coming from. They find the alien ship.

Page 30. The creature on Kane’s mask. They take it back to their ship to the infirmary.

Page 30–40. The first attempt at taking the creature off his mask, or trying to kill it. Fails.

Page 45. Creature is dead. Kane is back.

Page 50. They leave the planet. The ship is in hyperspace.

Page 55. Kane’s chest bursts, the creature is on board. Ripley comes up with a plan — catch it and eject it from the ship. Brett dies. The plan fails because the creature is huge now.

Page 65. New plan — they try fire. Fails. Dallas dies.

Page 75. They trap the creature but it escapes with help from Ash. We see him straight up lie for the first time.

Page 80. Reveal — Ash is a robot. They pull his plug. And they go to kill the creature.

Page 85. New plan — blowing up the ship. But creature kills Parker and Lambert. Ripley goes to the escape pod. The plan fails again.

Page 95. Ripley is in the escape pod, Narcissus. But the creature is there with her. New plan — She ejects it to space. Successful. The end.

Walter Hill and David Giler put an end to the story but not Ridley Scott. For him, the ending is — last plan, successful. Or is it? The cat is with her…

And there are two other plots other than trying to kill the alien creature; Ripley is trying to discover Ash’s intention and Ash is trying to save the creature. These to subplots continue in the background till they connect to the actual plot in the end.

**4. Real intentions and subtexts.**

The character, Ash is a robot. But the script doesn’t reveal it until the end. Until that point, the script gives hints about him. Ash always acts weird, and his actions and words seem always on trying to protect the creature.

Conflict between Ripley and Ash. Ripley tries to discover his intention.

**5. The positioning of the protagonist, Ripley in the story.**

She is the one who has the moral compass. She wants to follow the standard procedure and doesn’t want to take Kane on board. But Ash doesn’t listen to her and opens the hatch.

She follows Ash, and question his intentions. She is the one who always comes up with a plan. And she is the one who survives in the end.

I recommend reading this holy bible of screenwriting.

**ALIEN** by **Walter Hill** and **David Giler** based on the screenplay by **Dan O’Bannon**, story by **Dan O’Bannon** and **Ronald Shusett** [[PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1z9IAmguHggwhs1WZLRFtsZ-eOFZUwyst/view?usp=sharing)] — **for educational purposes only.**
