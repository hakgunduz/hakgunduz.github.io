This week I wanted to choose an old screenplay. Since I have a fascination about **Sidney Lumet**, I chose one of his films. He somehow always finds great stories and storytellers. He worked with **David Mamet** on **The Verdict**(1982) and **Paddy Chayefsky** on **Network**(1975). And on this week’s script **Dog Day Afternoon**, he worked with **Frank R. Pierson** — screenwriter of **Cool Hand Luke**(1967).

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*X50nZNuy1QRZNxMsbqSqlA.jpeg)

First of all it’s a masterful movie in every level and one of the boldest screenplays ever written. It’s a story of a man who robs a bank to pay for his lover’s sex change operation; it turns into a hostage situation and a media circus.

So if we dive into the screenplay, one of the first things I noticed was the protagonist’s name. It’s **JOHN** on the screenplay and **SONNY** in the movie. I can clearly see why they changed that. Sonny is more soft, more unisex. And it gives you different vibe than John. John has a dark side on the screenplay and you believe his wife when he says John is making his life hell. But in the movie, Sonny is more gentle. He has a good spirit and a vibe. And you, as audience have hard time believing his wife when he says Sonny is not a good guy.

Sonny never does anything bad or puts gun on people in the movie. But in the screenplay, John sometimes goes berserk but than notices and apologizes. And we have a glimpse of the other version of him. But this is not a case in the movie. Film portrays him as good and gentle spirit all the time. You root for him till the end.

And this is interesting, **you root for him till the end.** But he’s not particularly a good guy. In fact, he is the bad guy in this situation. He is the guy who is robbing the bank. He is the guy who is taking people as hostages.

And **SAL, the other robber.** He’s more violent and talkative in the screenplay. He’s nineteen. The gun in his hand makes him horny and he wants to use it. They gang-banged him in the jail so he can’t turn back to there.

In the movie, he’s not nineteen. He’s ready to use the gun because he’s under enormous pressure but he doesn’t crave for it. Instead, he gets a hold of himself all the time. And the movie never reveals his background.

**THE COPS** are more violent and ready to use their guns too in the screenplay. They’re ready to kill them all. There’s even a scene with MORETTI and the COMMISSIONER where they make a bet on whether if they kill the hostages or not.

**THE CROWD** are more on Sonny’s side in the movie. They yell out with him saying — *put the gun down.*But in the screenplay, crowd yell out saying — *faggots, faggots.*

And script sometimes shows the individuals of the crowd. This also didn’t see the final cut and I think it’s a spot on decision. Because it kills the overall tension every time.

This script is a great example of a character study. Because you see they fall in a dead end very beginning of the screenplay. They’re under enormous pressure all throughout the story and have to find a way to get out of it. This pushes them further and further to make difficult decisions. And each decision unravels new aspects of their character. Sonny chooses not to surrender and comes up with a plan.

Cops are laying a trap in the screenplay. There’s no scene like that in the movie. So when FBI GUY says his plan to Sonny, it’s nerve-wrecking for us and we start to anticipate what’s to come.

There’s wedding footage of John and Ernie in the screenplay but it didn’t make to final cut.

John laughs a lot, pantomimes kiss a lot — not Sonny.

Sal doesn’t believe John in the screenplay. He says he’s gonna sell him anyway.

It’s Ernie(John’s wife)’s birthday in the screenplay — not in the movie. They kiss in front of the crowd in the screenplay. There’s no scene like that in the movie.

John says he’s a fuck-up, outcast in the screenplay — Sonny doesn’t say anything like this. He believes in himself and he’s making all this because he has a lot of pressure from lots of things in his life. He never blames anybody else. Takes full responsibility.

Screenplay mentions money in the last scene. You already forgot about money in the movie by that. Your concerns are with their well-being.

There’s this bit in the movie, where they’re in the bus and Driver wants Sal to put his gun up repeatedly. But Sal keeps on pointing at him. That was my favorite bit in the movie but I couldn’t find it in the screenplay. There’s no scene like that.

And Driver misses at the first shot. He kills Sal in the second and he dies on John’s lap. John screams — *kill me — kill me!*

In the movie, Sonny says — *don’t shoot me.*

The movie ends with this scene but not the screenplay. There’s interview scenes with people in the crowd. There’s scenes where John’s wives talk. There’s a scene where his mother talks. There’s scenes on TV.

The screenplay ends with the weather channel. And I thing it’s meta because this is a true story…

I recommend reading Dog Day Afternoon screenplay. It’s a great script.
