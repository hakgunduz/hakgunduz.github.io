Disturbing, distressing, startling, spooky, creepy, chilling, bizarre… I can’t find the word. Nothing compares the feeling that this movie made me.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*hhUNlU4uohDLUeZ98pSSqg.jpeg)

(Spoilers)

The movie opens with the miniature house. We look at it, and the camera gets closer we realize it’s not a miniature but Peter’s room. His father wakes him up to go to the funeral. Grandma’s funeral. And this is not the only thing that’s startling in the opening sequence. Charlie’s tree house, and mannequins… Weird people in the funeral home. The sigil ornament on Grandma’s neck. Annie’s eulogy. We see some weird stuff that we don’t know yet is out there. It alters your reality about the world we’re in. It messes you up and you start to expect everything on the edge of your seat. At least I was…

It builds up the tension so well, and because your reality of the world altered the tension builds up better. When her teacher gets angry at Charlie a pigeon hits the window, breaking its neck. Then, she goes to cut its head off…

Another thing about the screenplay, Annie’s monologues. Annie has three big monologues that she reveals every bit of her past and her emotions. That felt kind of cheesy to me. But I was okay with it because the timing of the monologues. First, the eulogy and it doesn’t reveal much but makes you more curious about her past and her mother. Second one is in the scene where she goes to the support group and she breaks up in there. And the other one with the character Joan. And she breaks up in there too. But again, it felt cheesy to me. It could’ve been better if they show us those revelations somehow other than telling. But I’m guessing, this made them work more on the other startling details which I loved so much.

About the narrative, the character wants something. But someone or something gets in the way of him/her getting it. And the character needs to learn/grow in this journey to overcome this obstacle and get what s/he wants.

You don’t see it in there do you? Oh, it’s there in this movie as well. Big time.

Only one twist in this story is that Annie — and at the same time we — learn about the antagonistic force later in the screenplay. She fights the forces but doesn’t know what’s in the way. This makes the screenplay a good horror screenplay. The unknown, the subtle startling clues hit you where you don’t expect.

Annie’s family past haunts her. And her desire is to protect her family from her haunted past. She establishes that in the monologue at the support group. And in order for that, she needs to accept her past which we know she or any other her family members don’t do ever. She says this, says that no one accepts their fault to her son at her last big monologue. She blames others but she doesn’t accept either. And after this point, she starts to learn to accept. But first she needs to believe the spiritualism is real. And with that, a tool for the antagonistic force, the character Joan helps her see the truth about her past.

And the pressure point, the ticking clock of the screenplay is the Asher Gallery. She has a deadline on her work of miniature houses. And it comes up at every emotional turning point of the screenplay. First message from the gallery makes her gets angry at Peter about going to the party. And she makes him take his sister, Charlie with him. And the night ends up Charlie being dead. Other two post-it scenes in Annie’s workshop both relates to Annie going to see Joan. And the last one comes after the scene — where they talk to her dead daughter, Charlie’s spirit — and the phone call from the Asher Gallery makes her destroy all the work she has done — the biggest turning point for her.

While protagonists selfish journey continues, the evil antagonistic plan reveals itself as well. Everything we see till the last act turns out to be building for the climax. Everything pays off. Usually, this applies to every screenplay. But in this screenplay, the climax is a huge reveal and a kind of huge reversal as well. Turns out, Annie is a tool for the King Paimon, because of her family past. And unconsciously she made all the arrangements in order for King to reborn. Including digging up her mother’s grave. She fights it anyway but the antagonist defeats the protagonist in the end. And her headless body lures Peter to the King Paimon(the real villain in this story).
