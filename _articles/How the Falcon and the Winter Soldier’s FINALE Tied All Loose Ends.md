Before you start reading, I want to say that this content involves major **SPOILERS**. So, please stop now. Go watch the finale and then please come back.

The Falcon And The Winter Soldier was a great example of how a TV series could be something much more using only a little. It was concise, full of depth, and emotion and gave us great action scenes. I never thought I would miss the character Sam so much. I felt bittersweet when it ended.

Despite the fact the I was pretty damn excited for the season finale, I noticed another thing while watching it. And that was about how they handled each topic, theme, and sub-plot.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*0mVx9O_HJc5_cwNK1WSn4w.jpeg)

Here is the list of things I noticed.

- The way Sam faces the conflict.

This was the most important thing for me. I never thought much of Steve Rogers until he said “I’m not gonna fight you.” to Winter Soldier. That was the moment the character became so much more compared to just being just a super-soldier.

In Steve Rogers’ case, it was his friend. But in Sam’s case, it was about this girl who fights for what she believes in. Sam understands her. He knows how complicated the problem is and how the system and the constitutions brought them to this point. So, he decides not to fight her. He believes in her. And this is the moment where Sam becomes Captain America. Because being Cap is not about fighting, being Cap is about protecting. It’s why the symbol is a shield rather than a weapon.

Steve Rogers was always a symbol of will. He was the one you always counted on. And now, with The Falcon and the Winter Soldier, Marvel brought the complications of the real world onto the fiction and Sam is becoming a person who we always count on.

So yeah, Bucky. Steve was right about Sam. So, he was right about you also.

- Winter Soldier. Bucky’s notebook.

This was one another thing, Bucky was struggling to cope with his dark past throughout the series. But with the help of Sam, he got onto the right track and learned to make amends.

And in the finale of the season, he had a moment where he saved a bunch of people. One even thanked him for it.

- U.S. Agent. His chance of redemption.

The moment where he had a choice between revenge and redemption was so cool. Not gonna lie, one part of me wanted to see the version where he took the choice of revenge. It would’ve been so interesting to see if he got there. But after he becomes U.S. Agent I know we will see lots of interesting scenes.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*MvyMXs0SFZ605dKdgPs1qA.jpeg)

At first glance, I hated John Walker, but he has become one of my favorite characters in the series. He is a deeply flawed and complicated man. And it’s going to be so exciting to see whether if he gets his redemption or becomes even more of a villain in the series.

- Karli Morgenthau’s end. Her helplessness.

I thought it was so cool how they portrayed her end as the symbol of **helplessness** of people who suffer because of the government and the constitutions.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*o_nSgOCARuqIS_POCsxgZw.jpeg)

- Action scenes.

The action scenes were basically a lesson on how you can show what we have seen all the time in a new way. Each action sequence had this idea in its essence. Again, never thought a pair of wings could be so goddamn cool.

Overall, I enjoyed the hell out of how they came up with this concise structure. Things were deeply deeply deeply complicated. And they masterfully grifted them into each other.

I am going to miss Sam. But I am also excited for Loki. Marvel’s new TV series brought a new level of complexity into these stories and characters, and I’m all here for it.
