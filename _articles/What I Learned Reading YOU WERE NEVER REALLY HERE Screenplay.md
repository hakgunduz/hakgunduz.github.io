First of all, I didn’t like the movie at all.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*bRMJZ3Tg4cSL44ojuVXoWQ.jpeg)

(spoilers)

It starts with close-ups… I patiently waited for the close-ups to end but it never stopped. Watched the whole movie in close-ups. The movie doesn’t give you enough time to breathe. I don’t know if it’s deliberate or not — but I didn’t like it.

I felt like the movie follows this close-up style just for the stylistic purposes. It doesn’t have anything to do with the story. It’s just fractured and all over the place — all the time.

I know, I know — JOE can’t breathe sometimes. There are scenes where Joe escapes from the world to a closet and tries to breathe in there. It makes sense but only it works. And I don’t think it works. At least it didn’t work for me. It shadowed over my investment in the story. I felt detached all the time and watched the movie as an outsider. I didn’t feel anything while watching it.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*0VpfwRTL1zwpwB5Jq94Gxg.jpeg)

Started reading the screenplay expecting to be invested in the journey this time. Because I liked the premise a lot. But the screenplay was the same for me. It made me feel the same. It too is fractured and all over the place.

There are minor changes but the movie and the screenplay are almost the same. Except for JOE’S daydreaming scenes near the end of the movie and the ending scene at Road Side Diner.

- The movie ends with JOE and NINA at the Road Side Diner. NINA leaves him for a second and because of that JOE shots himself in the head. Then, NINA comes back from the toilet and sees JOE (alive) is waiting for her. And they leave together. It’s JOE and NINA’S story. The story of him saving her.
- The screenplay ends with JOE alone at the Road Side Diner. JOE again shots himself — I don’t know maybe just the hell of it — then sees NINA and his Mother in his daydream. Then he wakes up as waitress filling his cup of coffee. He gets on the wheel on his rental car. There are lots of weapons in the backseat. And drives off to save another soul from trouble. It’s JOE’S story. The story of him becoming some kind of modern hero.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*56_XVd6pcYZsAzPrH1C-0Q.jpeg)

One of the biggest struggles for me was this: JOE almost always knows what to do, where to look and it pays off all the time. Nothing pushes him in the action and the plot. He has inner struggles of course. And every time he does something good, something bad happens right after. But movie doesn’t make you feel that much of it. It feels off. Even if you don’t question it, you say: Something is not right here… And you start caring less and less.

Takeaways from the screenplay.

It shows how to direct on the page. The screenplay reads detail by detail all the time and it becomes close-ups on the finished movie.

It became one of the ‘learn from the bad movies-screenplays’ situation for me. I learned that I should never shadow over the main story and the main emotional drive with stylistic purposes. It all comes later. First, focus on the story. See if it works. Make sure it works. And don’t shadow over it with something else.
