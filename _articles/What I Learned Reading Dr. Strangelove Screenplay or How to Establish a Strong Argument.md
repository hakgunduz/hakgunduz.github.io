It’s written by Stanley Kubrick and Terry Southern based on the book “Red Alert” by Peter George.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*nlEt-t6JXzV6G_-Duh1zGA.jpeg)

When I watched it this time I wondered how did he establish the main ingredients of storytelling to this plot. Because it doesn’t feel like we see something like this every day. It’s different.

But more I think about it more I understood that it’s not that different. There’s a character who wants something and there’s an antagonistic source gets in the way of getting it.

What’s different is the positioning of the protagonist and the antagonist in the story.

The movie and the screenplay open with Ripper ordering the bombers to attack. Maj. Kong wants to follow the order. Turgidson wants to use this opportunity to win the war. Protagonists who want nuclear war.

Kubrick used this tweak to say something with his film. He made us stick with their journey even though we’re not rooting for their desires. Kubrick is saying “**Watch; the one thing you’re certain will not happen, can happen in a paradoxical and absurd way.”**

Someone can come up and make it happen. A general like Ripper can exceed his authority and order a nuclear attack. A pilot like Maj. Kong can stubbornly take the bomb to Russians even though you want him to recall. It can happen.

And in the movie, the whole world try to stop them but they can’t in the end. Ripper and Kong win against all the odds.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*rb9tPBHw67LZH7hoiSa2lw.jpeg)

**Suspense**

It’s also a suspense masterpiece. We’re watching till the end on the edge of our seats whether if the nuclear bomb will go off or not. There’s is an actual ticking clock.

President tries everything. He sends his troops to Ripper’s base. He even gives the plans of the attack to Russians. His plans fail but he comes up with another plan every time. He tries till the end.

Mandrake tries too. He tries to get some sense into Ripper. When Ripper kills himself, he finds the recall code and tries to send it to the President. But someone or something gets in the way every time.

**The Argument**

The story builds around to this argument. Ripper orders to attack, President, can’t help but watch him do it. Maj. Kong rides down the bomb like a lunatic.

There’s also a diplomacy factor, the absurdity of it. One general using a simple slip-up in the program(Plan R) and ordering a nuclear attack. America and Russia trying hand in hand to stop him. Because in the end, it’s the whole world we’re talking about.

An attack on Russia triggering the Doomsday Machine, because they were going to announce it next Saturday but they didn’t. Another slip-up that will end the all life on earth.

It’s all a joke.

You can see the argument in every beat of the story and also in the structure.

Kubrick uses this one brick of an argument and builds a movie with it, using the same argument again and again in a different context. Every second of the movie is made out of it. It’s solid.

**If we take one brick and look at it.**

The scene where Mandrake finds the recall code but can’t take it to the President because of Colonel ‘Bat’ Guano wants him to surrender.

He’s not someone who wants a nuclear war also, but the circumstances make him do this. Guano wants him to surrender, because that’s the order.

Mandrake has found the code but Guano gets in the way. Mandrake tries to get some sense into him also, explains the situation but Guano doesn’t believe him. Mandrake pushes his chance because a nuclear war is about to begin. He gets what he wants, a phone call to President. But lines are dead. He goes to a phone booth but he’s short of change. A nuclear war is about to start simply because this guy doesn’t have enough change. Then, he orders Guano to blast that coke machine off. Guano resists arguing that it’s a private property.

Mandrake doesn’t expect one of his own to get in the way of him stopping the nuclear war. But it happens. He doesn’t expect to not reach out to the President because he’s short of change. But it happens. When he tells Guano to blast the coke machine off he doesn’t expect him to resist him but it happens. It all can happen.

The structure of this scene says something, it’s built to say something. It’s not just a beat in the story where Mandrake finds the code and recalls the bombers. The scene is built to say exactly what the whole movie says.

If you look at any other scene, it’s the same. Look at the famous scene where Peter Sellers says**“Gentlemen, you can’t fight in here this is the war room!”**

No one expect from anyone to say something like this but it happens. That one line is the whole movie is about.
