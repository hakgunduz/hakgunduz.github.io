---
title: "Living my own life"
date: 2026-11-25
---

I saw a video the other day about two ways to cook.

You can follow a recipe. Step by step, and you get the dish.

Or you can walk into the kitchen and try things. Taste. Change something. Make it again. Slowly you find out what you like, and you end up with a dish that's yours.

The second way takes longer. But you live more of it. You discover your own taste along the way.

It stuck with me, because I've been doing the second one with stories for a while now.

I'm trying to figure out what makes a good book, a good story, through what I read and watch. When something grabs me, I stop and ask: why am I enjoying this moment? Why does it pull me in? Why do I care what happens next?

That's me tasting the dish.

And since I'm writing my own book, the next question is: how do I put what I've tasted into it? How do I get to a book I'd love to read?

After my therapy session today, the idea went one step further.

For most of my life, I've followed recipes. I'd look at other people's lives, see something they had and I didn't, and work out the recipe. Do this, then this, and you'll get there.

Writing my book showed me another way. And it works for living my own life, too.

It sounds simple. Other people's lives are theirs. My life is mine. I knew that already. Now I come back to it with a little more understanding.

Everything I see in other people, everything I go through, everything I learn, those are ingredients I'm tasting. Not every one has to be a lesson. Some I just get to know. Some I try for a while.

Then I use them to cook the meal I actually want to eat.
