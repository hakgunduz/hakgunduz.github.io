---
title: "A working theory on how to create unputdownable books"
date: 2026-11-07
---

- Everything should be about the main character, literally. What happens in that world should relate to them somehow. We need to care about them and what happens to them.
- The world needs a clear structure so readers can anticipate what might happen. Events need to connect through causality.
- [Stories are about change](https://hakgunduz.github.io/notes/stories-are-about-change/). Each character has a “theory of control”: an understanding of how the world works and who they are. The story tests that theory so the character changes.
- Scenes need a delicate balance of surprise, suspense and curiosity. For me, suspense is what makes a book unputdownable. Fear about what might happen next drives the pace.
- My working theory is that orientation, anticipation and resolution apply to the whole experience of a book, including how it’s pitched. Orientation starts before the first page: with the blurb, the elevator pitch or wherever someone first encounters the book. These shape what people anticipate, and the story eventually resolves those expectations through surprise, reversal or payoff.
- Will Storr describes stories in terms of survival, connection and status. Combining them can make a story stronger, especially when all three are present.
- The story follows the character’s change. Will Storr’s five-act structure is a way of thinking about how that change unfolds. Multiple plotlines should interconnect.
- Description and atmosphere should support suspense, curiosity and pacing. What we feel for the characters, whether love or hate, matters too.
