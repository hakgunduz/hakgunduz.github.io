“I know you don't really believe in that sort of thing, but I think my brother's ghost is still in my life,” Ediz said.

Nejat didn't know how to respond. “I want to tell you something important,” Ediz said, pulling him aside. Then those words spilled out of his mouth.

Nejat's right hand was scratching the back of his left arm under his elbow in a way that Ediz could not see, creating new cracks in his already cracked skin.

Ediz had lost his older brother on vacation with his family two weeks ago. A day that was going so well turned into a disaster as the weather suddenly changed. Although everyone was out of the sea, his brother didn't care much about the weather and wanted to swim a little more.

He was always like that. He was someone who could enjoy life at any moment, run to Ediz's needs no matter what, and have fun with him. He wanted to show Ediz who was frustrated with the bad weather that they could have fun in rainy weather.

He made Ediz laugh by catching the raindrops falling into the sea and pretending to drink them. At this very moment, he did not see the big wave coming behind him. He lost his balance in the water when the wave hit him and was dragged into the depths.

The waves that sneaked up on his brother that day did not stop. They repeatedly crashed onto the shore in order to achieve their goals. Meanwhile, Ediz's brother was drifting deeper as he wanted to surface.

In front of Ediz's eyes, this moment had lasted for hours. Anyone who tried to help was either unable to reach him or, after one attempt, was helplessly returning to shore.

After a while, Ediz could not see his brother at all. As the roaring of the air and the pounding of the sea did not cease, they were only able to extricate his body until the morning of the next day.

Nejat, who was devastated by the terrible news immediately came to see his best friend when they returned to the city. As they were close with Ediz, on the contrary, he did not know his brother. They didn't get much of a chance to get together. But he always felt like he knew him because Ediz wouldn’t stop talking about him.

The silence between them was disturbing Nejat. He felt the need to say something.

“You buried him next to your grandfather?” he could say. He was sitting with his arms wrapped around his chest and hunched forward. It was like he had a ton of weight on his shoulders. The fingernails of his hand had dug into his arm and had even begun to bleed, but he hadn't noticed it.

“Yes,” said Ediz.

"Both the same name, side by side, anyone who sees it will think it's a mistake," said Nejat. He regretted what he had said before the words even came out of his mouth. He wanted to make his friend laugh.

“It's true, they're not even a year apart,” Ediz said.

"How are your parents?"

“Managing there.”

“Your brother was renting a house, wasn't he? What did you do about the flat?" asked Nejat. He continued to regret everything he had said. He cursed the way his mind worked at that moment.

“I don't know,” Ediz said.

“Would you like to take a walk?”

“No,” Ediz said. His eyes never drifted elsewhere, focused on the gray stones on the ground.

“Maybe it will be good,” Nejat said. He couldn't help Ediz recover from this. The helplessness in him had begun to bother him so much that it was overpowering his regret for what came out of his mouth.

“What are you going to do?” he continued. “Life is like that, unfortunately. We will continue somehow. Don't be afraid, this too shall pass.”

Ediz continued to study the lines between the gray stones on the floor.

“You just need some time,” Nejat said. He was hunched forward as he continued to make his elbow bleed. He felt as if his words weighed on his own shoulders.

He was walking around the room in his mind, trying to shake him off. "Get ahold of yourself!" he wanted to shout. However, both of them could do nothing but stare at the floor of gray stones and the irregular lines between them.

A few days later, Ediz took Nejat to somewhere quiet and talked about his brother's ghost.

“I am back to work this week. I went to the toilet for a while while I was at the office yesterday. When I first entered, I thought the sensor faucet was broken. Then it worked on its own again,” Ediz said.

"It's probably broken," said Nejat.

“Yes, I thought so too. I didn't dwell much on it. But today I asked the cleaning guy again. He said it's not broken, it never worked on its own all day. Then I entered and it started working immediately.”

“It's just a coincidence,” Nejat said. The cracks in his skin had increased, and they had formed small maps in various parts of his body.

“Interestingly, my brother came to the office to visit me once. We went to the toilet together. The way he used the tap while washing our hands was funny to me. Once he was soaping his hand, holding it underwater, and then pulling it out and cleaning between his fingers. What followed was the most interesting part. He kept rubbing his hand again and again until he was able to get the soap out of his hand, repeatedly putting his hand under the water and immediately pulling it out.”

"What does that mean, I don't understand."

“Then I paid attention. My brother always washed his hands that way. If the faucet had no sensors, the water was constantly flowing, but the pattern of washing hands was always the same.”

“Well?"

“The faucet was flowing the same way!” cried Ediz.

Nejat felt as if his chest was tight. It was as if a dark shadow had hovered deep in his stomach for days.

After a moment of silence. “Ediz, you are having a hard time. It is normal for you to think and feel such things…”

“Nejat…” Ediz interrupted. “I say my brother was there. Maybe even here with us.”

All Nejat felt was the darkness deep in his stomach. He had been wondering where the things he said came from for a few days. Because ever since he got the news, he felt like he was nothing but a dark pit.

“I know, he's following me,” Ediz continued.

“Please don't get caught up in this. Please. If you think about things like that, you'll never be able to recover."

“Who said I wanted to recover!” cried Ediz. “How would you know what I want!”

“I want you to get better,” Nejat said.

“I'm better with my brother,” Ediz snapped. Something broke between them that day. However, Nejat never stopped being next to Ediz. He was just trying to say less.

Every time they talked about his brother's ghost, the darkness inside Nejat was getting bigger. He went to the doctor for the cracks in his skin. He changed medicine for the third time, but still, nothing helped.

A few days later, he was washing his face, as he did every morning, and then applied cream to his arm. When he brought his right hand to the faucet, his hand suddenly twitched. It was like he couldn't control his muscles. The spot just below his right elbow, which he always scratched had become completely numb.

His fingers opened and closed on their own. He slowly returned to the living room and only then could he hear his phone ringing. When he took the phone in his hand, he saw that Ediz had called him three times and left many messages.

“I can't feel him,” one of the messages read. "My brother's ghost has disappeared."

Nejat called Ediz back. He was examining his arm. He thought about what the doctor said. His skin was regenerating itself. He studied the new skin coming out from under the cracks on his skin.

“My arm just twitched,” he said to Ediz on the phone.

They met in Ediz's attic that same night. It was the first time they'd been here since Ediz's father had cut off the electricity to the attic when they were both young. They carried a candle in their hands.

“Do you think it has anything to do with it?” ' asked Nejat.

“He might be trying to take over your body,” Ediz said.

Nejat thought that his friend might be feeling guilty over this. He was lost in the confusion of his experiences. He no longer felt like a dark pit, but as if he was the  darkness itself.

Ediz was standing in the small attic, pacing briefly. After a long silence when only his steps were heard.

“I don't know if he can hear me,” Ediz said. “But I will ask him to leave you alone.”

Nejat didn't know what to say. He could very well understand the situation his friend was in. The dark pit within him had returned again.

In the darkness, a force was pushing him from above and burying him deep into the pit. He felt the strange resistance of the dark pit on his face. The pit felt also like a swamp. He was being pulled down from the depths of his stomach.

As Ediz took short steps back and forth, Nejat wanted to hold his right arm by the elbow with his left hand and to close himself in himself, to disappear right there in the darkness.

Then he felt the presence of his right arm in his left hand. He soon realized that he could breathe. He didn't know that he had been breathing for days.

He took a deep breath. The darkness in his stomach was beginning to dissolve. He noticed his body completely.

He felt the living room advancing under the floor where he was sitting, the ground beneath it. Then he turned his awareness up and thought of the roof and the air that followed.

He pictured the sky in his mind. He took a deep breath again. He thought that the oxygen entering his body had healed the cracks in his skin.

What he felt before was stuck in the swamp inside him, but at that moment he thought that everything could flow out of his body.

“You don't need to say anything to your brother,” Nejat said.

Ediz stopped just as he took a step. In an instant, tears welled in his eyes. Nejat saw the tears falling from Ediz's eyes.

He fell down and started to cry. When Nejat went to him and put his hand on his shoulder, Ediz hugged him and continued to cry on his shoulders.

“Nejat, I lost my brother,” he said.
