---
title: "Mindsight"
date: 2023-01-22 18:05:46 +0000
---

His Mindsight must have been frozen because he was stuck in the middle of the intersection and was not moving at all.

At that moment, a scrapper who is passing by noticed the person in the middle of the street. He parked the wheelbarrow next to the garbage. He was going to examine the scraps of electronic gadgets. However, the person in the middle of the street caught his attention more.

He looked around. There were many cafes, people sitting in front of the cafes and chatting, and people walking quickly from the sidewalk to go to the market. Everyone wanted to enjoy the day on this lively weekend morning.

No one noticed the person who was frozen in the middle of the street. It wasn't a very busy street, though. If a courier motor or a taxi were on the street, he could have noticed and moved out of the way before the vehicles got close to him.

The frozen person looked in his direction. The scrapper turned his head, wondering where he was looking. He saw a sensor mounted on the building that monitored the intersection.

When the frozen person turned his head to the street, he saw the courier motor that was speeding towards him. It was a quiet one because it was an electric courier. At the last moment, he managed to move his legs and threw himself onto the pavement.

He saw a dying dog on the pavement. The dog was emaciated and looked like he wouldn't have the energy to eat even if the food was put in front of him. He turned his head and continued walking to the left, but at that moment he collided with someone.

“Look ahead, bro!” exclaimed the person in front of him. However, he himself didn't seem to be looking too far ahead, he seemed to be focused on his Mindsight.

“Sorry,” he said and continued on his way. As he walked along the pavement, his distracted attention suddenly focused on his Mindsight, and he was out of sight of the scrapper.

The scrapper had already started digging through the electronics next to the trash. He was digging out screens he found among them. He found several smartphones. He ripped the screens off in one motion and threw them in the wheelbarrow.

Then he managed to find several monitors and television screens. Most of them didn't even have a scratch on them. He could also hear the conversation of two people sitting in front of the cafe across the street as he continued his search among the garbage.

“I think it was a human fault again,” said the one in the brown sweater. “It was as if it suddenly became aware and made a decision with its free will.”

The person with a baseball cap sitting across from him made a noise indicating that he was listening. It was unclear whether he approved or not.

“Nobody understands how this works,” the guy in the brown sweater continued.

“Most people just accept it as is,” said the baseball cap.

"Aren't we already prisoners of our subconscious and instincts?" ' brown sweater asked. He didn't seem like he was expecting an answer.

“It's kind of problematic that it dictates our movements, though,” the baseball cap began.

The brown sweater cut him off. “Exactly, but you have to understand that this is a new technology. It may have affected the whole world at once, but it is still too early to fully understand it.”

“Oh, my tea is cold. I forgot I ordered it,” said the baseball cap. While trying to drink his tea, he had a hard time taking the glass to his mouth at first. He was grimacing when he took his first sip.

“A freer world,” brown sweater said.

“Do you think someone will go to jail for what happened?” said the baseball cap.

“I think it should because it's their fault,” brown sweater said. "But of course, it's in their self-interest to shift the focus to this new technology."

"What do you think he's thinking?" ' the baseball cap asked, pointing to the scrapper across the street.

“For him, Mindsight is probably something like Skynet,” brown sweater said.

The baseball cap started laughing.

“Does he expect someone from the future to come as well,” he said, and something else caught his attention.

“Is that stuff he collects 'screens'?” he asked.

“Yes,” brown sweater said.

“Are those things what they call screens?” baseball cap looked surprised.

“Yes, old technology. I used one when I was little… antique.” said the brown sweater. “He probably thinks we're stupid, that we're selling our souls,” he continued.

“What is he doing with the screens?” said the baseball cap.

“I have no idea,” brown sweater said. “I guess what they used to do.”

"Is that a stance?" A stance against Mindsight?”

"I don't think so. I think he's just poor.”

“Pity,” said the baseball cap. He had already turned his attention to his Mindsight and changed the subject.

After collecting as many screens as he could, scrapper pulled the wheelbarrow out of the parking lot and continued on his way. He made his way straight down the sidewalk towards the back streets outside the district.

As he walked, the number of people decreased, and the buildings he passed changed from brand new good-looking buildings to shabby and half-destroyed buildings.

When he got to his house, he clicked on the little screen on the door. He entered his password on the device, which looked like an old smartphone, and the door opened.

Inside, it looked more like a large warehouse with a high ceiling rather than a house. There were no windows. Living areas were separated by curtains attached to iron hangers with mobile legs.

Scrapper drove the wheelbarrow to the back of the house, behind all the curtains. Here he emptied the screens he had collected that day, on top of much other electronic waste piled up in a wall corner.

He headed for what looked like the living room. The seats were facing the huge TV that is mounted on the wall. Everywhere the scrapper stepped in was strewn with half-assembled devices that looked like attempts to design various electronic devices.

He hadn't covered the back of the gigantic television, hadn't tidied up the wires. Cables were sticking out in many places. There were a lot of cables running through the house in many different places.

Scrapper sat in the chair. He seemed to be thinking of something. On the sofa were pillows, sheets, and a quilt piled in a corner.

The television had been on since he had entered the house, but its volume was low. He took out a small screen from his pocket and tapped it a few times. The TV volume was turned up.

On the huge screen, a masked face was working on a device that looked similar to the devices that are around the scrapper's house in various forms.

"They're just slaves," said the mask on the screen. “They accept what is presented to them and throw their lives away. They sign contracts to surrender their lives and they don't even read those contracts. They are oblivious.”

While speaking, he was giving instructions and directed the audience about the design of the device he was working on.

“The ones behind Mindsight? Yes, they think we can't get the source code, but we are very close. For them, even being anonymous is a business move,” he said.

Scrapper grabbed the nearest screen and began to follow the instructions suggested by the masked man on the television.

“Yes, guys, if you follow the instructions I gave about the screen in my hand, the screen can get access to the Mindsight applications. You can reach our Freeeye community from the link in the bio.”

Scrapper reached the coffee table near him, grabbed a keyboard-like device, and held it in his lap. He pressed a few keys and an app interface popped up on the television screen.

In the right bar of the application, he saw that many profiles were active. He chose a person named SoulGazzsy from among the profiles and sent a message, “Freeeye screen is $8 now.”

He pressed a few more buttons and a notepad app opened. Various numbers were written in the notes inside. Among them, he found the line that read “113 screens, $439.” Deleted 113 and replaced it with 117. He then deleted the "439 dollars" next to it and made "463 dollars".

He grabbed the screen he was working on again in his hand. After looking at it for a while, he threw it on the pillow. He stood up and stretched.

He heard a beep. Confused, he headed towards the door. He stared at the small wall-mounted screen above and to the right of the door. He watched the camera pointing in front of the door. There was nothing.

He heard a noise and noticed that the sensor vacuum was coming fast toward his feet. He nearly knocked over the large plant pot in the corner of the hall when he pulled his foot away at the last moment and turned left.

The leaves of the potted plant were bent to the ground, and many of them were already yellowing and dry. Scrapper looked at the plant as if noticing it for the first time. Then he was distracted by the sound coming from the television.

“Soulgazzsy has invited you into the room,” the on-screen notice read. Scrapper headed for the keyboard on the couch.

Meanwhile, one of the large yellow leaves of the plant bent a little more towards the ground, broke off from its root, and fell to the ground.
