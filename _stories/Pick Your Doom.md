**Day 1**

I decided to pick my doom.

Since the day I saw Nelson who lost his mother, his state was always on my mind. He couldn't come to school for a few weeks after he heard the news. When he came, he was like a walking ghost. His connection with the existing world was cut off.

That day I made a decision. I was going to take control of my own life. Losing someone covered a large area among the misfortunes that could happen to me.

I thought about how it would feel to lose my mother, father, or brother. Honestly, it wasn't something I could fully comprehend. I had never lost anyone before. I felt like my life was wrapped in a protective spell.

When I thought about where the idea of this spell came from, I realized that I had never lost anyone in my life. I had grown up with their love, and I still felt their love. I chose a school close to my family for high school. I was even planning to do the same for college.

That's why when I saw Nelson, I was filled with the fear of losing that spell. I had to be prepared for it.

Losing my brother would probably be the worst. Because he had so much more to live for. We had dreams, his and mine. My parents had already lived what they were going to live. But my father was the person who made our family a family; without him, we would have constantly fought with each other.

I couldn't even imagine losing my mother... How could I prepare for that? Those days would come eventually. I wanted to be ready.

Before I even got to them, I had to prepare myself to lose my grandparents, my grandmother, and my grandfather.

Death was probably the greatest misfortune that could happen to me. It was so heavy that I couldn't even think of the other disasters.

"Death is no doom, it is salvation," a voice inside me said.

**Day 4**

What could be worse than death? It had been bothering me for the last few days.

If we were not old, we did not think about death. We couldn't see it. I thought that being able to see the coming of death would be worse. This meant a serious illness.

Being diagnosed with cancer or someone I love being diagnosed with cancer would be so difficult. The approaching inevitable end, the few remaining days spent in pain and weakness would be very terrifying.

Kidney failure, lung problems, or losing a limb would also be quite unpleasant. How could I prepare myself for these possibilities?

To prepare myself for the possibility of losing various limbs or senses, I decided to practice with the opposite hand or foot during the day. I closed my eyes while cooking in the kitchen.

I hoped that losing one sense would increase the effect of the others, as I had seen in martial arts movies. I had to be prepared for everything.

**Day 7**

The idea that I could continue my life as an individual despite the idea of losing my family had come to my mind. I had always thought about the strangeness of this bond that came with birth.

Without anyone asking, responsibility and love were imposed on me. Now I spend my days in the fear of losing my loved ones. Maybe I should distance myself from my family.

My efforts to enhance my senses continued. Every day, I tried to prepare myself by discovering new diseases or other misfortunes. While thinking about all these, I realized something that we all have and that we never even think about. I had never thought about the importance of the need for shelter until that day.

What if we lost our home? What if we were homeless? What if we couldn't pay our rent? Winter was approaching. My perspective towards the homeless people I saw on the streets had changed.

Was it worse to be homeless or to be sick?

**Day 10**

While preparing myself for the idea of being homeless for a few days, I was also planning what I could do. I wouldn't be homeless as long as I found a job.

Was not being able to find a job or not having any income an option? Could it be worse than getting sick or losing someone from the family?

When it first came to my mind, it had deeply shaken my entire being. Why had the idea of not being able to find a "job" concept that we produce for ourselves as humanity scared me so much?

I thought it might be because everything else was based on it.

I used to tell myself that there was no shame in any job. However, could I have to do a job that I would be ashamed of one day? Apparently, I had not fully embraced the idea that there was no shame in any job.

I had to start working on it. The multitude of misfortunes that I needed to prepare for had started to take over my daily life. Sometimes, even in classes, I found myself lost in involuntary thoughts and unable to follow the subject.

**Day 13**

The fear that could come from possible natural disasters filled me as much as the bad feelings caused by artificial disasters.

We could experience a landslide on the road while on vacation and get crushed under the soil. An earthquake could leave me jobless, orphan, and homeless.

The complexity of natural disasters, affecting more than one thing and humanity not having much control over them, caused me to call this phenomenon the biggest misfortune.

**Day 17**

So, what level of misfortune would it be to break up with your loved one who has been in your life for years?

Actually, I thought it could be like dying. The person you lived with, the person you were with, would disappear. The dreams you could achieve would evaporate into thin air.

But still, being healthy and continuing to live should be a good thing. So, I couldn't rank it too high compared to other misfortunes.

I was sure that when we were in the moment of separation, we felt that we were experiencing the worst thing in the world. I had not experienced such a big relationship yet, fortunately.

I left a note for myself here that I should not establish such a strong bond with anyone in the future.

**Day 22**

Could addiction be considered a misfortune? Actually, this phenomenon was something that occurred as a result of other misfortunes.

Losing someone you love or experiencing a great separation could push one towards addiction. Our biological predisposition towards addiction was a greater misfortune because each of us was inevitably experiencing a series of misfortunes in life. Someone prone to addiction was adding another misfortune on top of the others.

This situation, which was difficult to get out of, made me think a lot. Being prone to addiction was a great misfortune. I couldn't figure out how to put it in order on my list.

I would have to think about this again. I'm really tired now.

**Day 25**

Trauma could also be one of the misfortunes that could lead to addiction.

Trauma was a misfortune that cast a shadow over a person's entire life and could turn their sunny future into a cloudy or even foggy one. The connection between trauma and other misfortunes also made me think a lot. I could lose someone I loved in a bad car accident and have to live with the trauma of it at the same time.

I should develop a defense that fought against all disasters simultaneously rather than one by one.

"Death is not a disaster, it's salvation," the voice inside me repeated.

I think I was starting to understand this voice a little bit.

**Day 31**

I could have been living with a chronic illness or disability. I was grateful that many of them were congenital.

However, various chronic diseases or disabilities could develop over time that I could catch. There could be a problem in my stomach, spine, muscles, nervous system, or various parts of my body.

I thought that it would always be good to develop mentally and to do mental work against them. Even if I couldn't move my body, I could follow my dreams.

The feeling of shaping my dreams accordingly suddenly engulfed me like a dark cloud. This thought experiment that I had progressed logically had affected me so much for the first time.

Yet, I didn't even have a dream. However, the idea of a restriction that could come upon my dreams had shaken me. This was one of the biggest misfortunes that I had to be prepared for:

Preparation for my dreams.

**Day 43**

Preparing my dreams became one of the things that I struggled with the most. In fact, during this process, another misfortune came to my mind.

Feeling isolated, alone, and purposeless in life should also be a great misfortune. I had been experiencing this to a great extent lately.

I started to question the impact of this project that I had initiated.

"Death is salvation," the voice inside me kept repeating.

I had been repeating this more often lately.

Now, I understood. All these misfortunes disappearing at once meant death.

"If I suddenly disappeared, who would care about me? Maybe I should have prepared for this. Instead of preparing myself for possible misfortunes, I should have cut my ties with people and then vanished.

Why was life so dark? I’m tired of taking notes.

**Day 66**

I prepared a list for myself. A list of misfortunes.

I had been updating the list for the past three weeks. Every morning when I woke up, another misfortune was weighing heavily on me. The things that bothered me had turned into a series of events.

When one of them came to mind during the day, the others would rush to my mind at full speed. These ideas had started to create a physical weight on my shoulders.

I was writing on the last page of my notebook. The thought of getting a new one didn't seem like it would ever come true. I had been having difficulty even going to school in the past few weeks.

Maybe this could be the last entry.

**Day 79**

I couldn't get a new notebook, but I found an old notebook with a few blank pages.

I remembered an agreement I made with myself years ago. I had been trying to find that notebook for three days. I finally found it.

"The biggest disaster you could do to yourself would be to try to choose one of the disasters that could happen to you," I saw a note. It had a date next to it. I had written this six years ago.

Had I planned the agony I had been going through for the past few months, six years ago?

"Your worst habit is indecision," the notes continued. "Overcome your indecision..."

**Day 80**

I bought a new notebook.

While sitting on a bench, I wrote a sentence on the first page of my new notebook.

"Perhaps it would be a more blessed life to continue your days by denying the existence of misfortunes rather than living in fear.”
