*A dog disguised as a fox had jumped out of the garbage.*

*Kerim thought that the animal he saw out of the corner of his eye as he passed the garbage was an orange cat. After taking a few steps, he thought the animal's back was too big for a cat.*

*His eyes tried to move forward, but he couldn't move his feet. He wanted to move on with his life, but he couldn't get rid of the idea that he saw a fox. The orange feathers he saw must have belonged to a fox.*

*As soon as he turned and looked into the trash, the animal inside of it jumped out. It had the body of a dog and the nose and ears of a fox. Its hair was also short and orange-white in color. Its tail was a shape that widened and darkened towards the tip as if it had been added later.*

*It was unlikely that he would encounter a fox in the city where they lived. So he thought he should assume that the animal he saw was a dog. When their eyes met, he compared the animal's gaze to a wolf.*

*The magical beast began to move across the road and away. They were outside the city. There weren't many cars on the street. In this quiet night illuminated by street lights, Kerim decided to follow the animal he saw.*

“There's nothing really magical about it,” said Kerim, a red-haired high school student. He was always a calm person with strict principles.

Naim's excitement was suffocated.

He was a high school student whose hair began to turn gray at a young age. However, his cheerful nature made everyone question the reason for this. Until Kerim spoke, he explained what had happened to him that day in a very excited way.

When he woke up in the morning, he thought he wanted to adopt a cat, and he spent the whole day thinking about it. On the way back from work, he had heard a meowing sound on the road he passed every day and noticed a kitten stuck in the bushes.

In the evening of the same day, he met Kerim and told him about his experiences.

“Your mind is programmed to affirm what you think. It sees what you expect to see.” Karim continued.

“It's actually a bit of perceptual selectivity. You prepared your mind for this and that's why you noticed that cat."

Naim was listening with a grimace.

“People always say that whatever energy you send to the universe, you will find its reward, for example, this has reflections in various societies. This is exactly the same thing.”

Kerim took a sip of his beer. "They're preparing their minds for what they expect, so their brains do what a brain is supposed to do and recreate the reality they're told."

As always, Naim had switched to passive listening behavior. Because he knew Kerim was going to talk about this for a while.

*He was doing research on his phone about the animal he saw while walking on the road. Among the options he found, the Ethiopian wolf was the most suitable.*

*However, he could not understand how an Ethiopian wolf could come to a small town in Turkey.*

*He was trying not to miss the orange wolf. They began to move towards the outskirts of the city. He was a little scared, but his curiosity kept him walking.*

“Who is coming?” ' Karim asked.

“The gang will be there. This time we will play in the garden of our high school,” said Naim.

"No. It's English day on Mondays, I can't break my routine now,” Kerim said.

“You can work tomorrow, this gang doesn't get together every day,” said Naim.

“We will be back late, and tired. I'm going to have to take a shower. I'd better stay and study,” said Kerim.

“After the game, we can sit and watch the stars,” said Naim.

“Unfortunately, the city lights won’t allow for that much,” Kerim said.

“I know a place,” said Naim. “It is especially beautiful under the moonlight. I even saw something moving in the bushes the other day. I want to go there again in case a werewolf comes out.”

“Naim, werewolves are fictional creatures,” Kerim said.

“Yes, but where did the fiction come from? You think we know the past very well, but maybe there are things we don’t know. Maybe there is a mysterious animal that inspired the werewolf. And we will see it. How do you know?"

“I know,” said Kerim.

*He had followed the wolf outside the city. The trees were very sparse. Therefore, he was able to continue to follow the wolf without losing sight of it.*

*After the wolf advanced a little, it looked back as if it was sure that Kerim was following him, and then continued on its way. Non-verbal communication began to be established between them.*

*He had a feeling that went against everything Kerim had learned and thought until that day. He, too, could perceive this communication.*

“Are we going to study history?” ' Kerim asked over the phone. He was talking to Naim.

“No,” said Naim. “I have some things to do tonight.”

“Can you be able to find time to study? I can give you my notes if you want. Let's talk later?" insisted Kerim.

"No thanks. I can study by myself,” Naim snapped.

He had never acted like this before. Kerim could not understand the reason for the coldness between them. After hanging up the phone, he got ready to go to the gym and left the house.

On his way to the gym, he passed a trash can on the sidewalk at the side of the street. First, he heard the noises coming from within, and then he glanced into the garbage as he passed by.

He had seen an animal with orange fur…

*The wolf disappeared behind the small hill in an instant. When Kerim followed it over the hill, he saw the wolf scratching something. As if playing a game.*

*The silhouette of the wolf in the moonlit night captivated Kerim’s eyes.*

*A bird took off from the tree just beyond the wolf. Without even flapping its wings, it glided toward the wolf and landed on its back.*

*When the bird's face brightened a little, Kerim compared this animal he saw to a hawk. However, it was not as big as a hawk, nor did it have a hawk-like body structure.*

*It was a bird with the face of a hawk and the body of an owl. Its fur was gray in color and white in places.*

Kerim ran to Naim's house. He knocked on his door.

He was incredibly excited to tell him what he had seen…
