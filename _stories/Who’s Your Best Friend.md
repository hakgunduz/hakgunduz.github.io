---
title: "Who’s Your Best Friend?"
date: 2023-04-16 19:15:06 +0000
---

I thought I would have fun filling out the survey I found. When I got to the eighth question, I was stumped. "What is your best friend's name?"

For a while, we stared at each other with this question. "What are they going to do with my best friend's name in the survey?" I didn't understand anything. Not being able to answer this question also ruined my enthusiasm for the survey.

I was bored. I opened my computer to distract myself. Because of the survey, nothing on the internet seemed interesting anymore.

I turned off the computer and looked around. The pieces of furniture were scattered around the house. It had only been five days since I moved in. The movers had left my old things in a disorderly manner. I had also not unpacked the new items yet.

The new things I bought were furniture that needed to be assembled. I usually wasn't very good at doing it alone. But I couldn't remember a piece of furniture that I didn't assemble alone.

I texted my friend Nuri. "We're getting together, come over," he replied.

I didn't even think of asking for help when I wrote the message. But after this reply, I thought about how quickly we could assemble and organize the furniture if the whole team came to my house. I quickly dismissed this idea.

I didn't want to burden people. I couldn't invite people to my house to work while they planned to have fun. What would they think of me? I didn't want to be that person everyone hated.

When I entered my friend's house, everyone seemed to have already arrived. I saw that various games were set up. Board games, drinking games, and many other games were ready.

After Nuri came out of the kitchen and gathered everyone together. "Tonight we will play various games. But first, everyone needs to split into groups," he said. Then he started to explain the games and the night's overall plan.

Suddenly, everyone started to talk to their friends and form groups. I felt left out. I didn't know who to go to and ask to be a part of their group. Although I occasionally hung out with these friends, I didn't feel very close to any of them.

As one of the last remaining people, I walked towards the wall and started watching others while holding a beer. Nuri noticed this and assigned the remaining people to the existing groups.

I had been included in a group, but I could tell even as I approached them I wasn't wanted. Even though they smiled and welcomed me, I thought they didn't want me in their group.

The games continued all night. Although I secretly wanted to win, I did what I was told because I was afraid to direct the other people in my group. At the end of the night, our group was the losing one, and we had to pay for all the drinks.

The next morning, I was awakened by the delivery man calling me. The weight set I had ordered three days ago had arrived.

As soon as the delivery man answered the phone, he asked me which floor I was on. When I told him I was on the fourth floor, he said that the package was too heavy for him to carry upstairs. He said he would leave it at a store downstairs and then hung up.

I didn't understand which store he was talking about, so I quickly got dressed and went downstairs. He had left the package at the optician's store below our building. As soon as I walked into the store, the optician looked at me in a bothered way and said, "The delivery man dropped off your package and left."

"I'm sorry," I said. "I didn't tell him to leave it here. I thought they would bring it upstairs."

"You can take it," he said, pointing to the package's location.

"The package is too heavy. If it's okay with you, can I come back tomorrow with a friend and carry it upstairs?" I asked.

The optician seemed even more bothered by my question. He looked at me as if he wanted to say no. But he must have realized that I couldn't carry the package, so he said quietly, "Okay."

"You can come and pick it up tomorrow morning," the optician added.

"Okay, thank you very much," I said and left the store. The optician's behavior annoyed me. I felt like I couldn't breathe as I walked upstairs. When I got home, I took a few deep breaths in a row. The mess of things at home bothered me a lot. I wanted to rearrange some furniture and tidy up some corners of the house. However, the couches were too heavy, so I collapsed onto the floor cushion, out of breath.

Even the curtains weren't hung up. I wanted everything to be organized at once. Every step I took in the house made me feel worse.

When I opened my computer, I saw the survey again. The same question was staring at me.

"What is your best friend's name?"

I put the computer aside and picked up my phone. I saw a message from my friend Zeynep inviting me to coffee.

I wanted to get away from the mess of the house. Having an excuse to go out made me happy. I started walking toward the coffee shop where Zeynep was while avoiding the optician’s shop. His behavior had stressed me out so much. I couldn't understand why he couldn't wait one day for the package to be carried upstairs. Usually, shopkeepers would help with such things. I had encountered the most stubborn shopkeeper by chance. I couldn't afford to come across people like him with so many other things to deal with.

When I met Zeynep, I wanted to forget everything I had been through until that moment. I hadn't seen her for a while. I didn't even think about asking for help with carrying the weights. All I could think about was the stress the optician had caused me.

"I'll have a single shot latte with cream on top," I said as Zeynep was going to order our drinks. She stared at me blankly.

"Cream?" she said. "Do you mean you want a caramel latte or a mocha or something like that?"

After asking her question, she answered it herself by saying, "Okay," and went to order. I was sure she would get my order wrong.

"I thought, 'Let's have a caramel latte today.' But generally, this drink seemed too sweet to me. I was sure I couldn't finish it.

And it turned out as I had thought. Zeynep had come back to me with a caramel latte. After taking a sip, I realized I couldn't finish this drink.

However, I didn't speak up. Because Zeynep had already started telling me about her ex-boyfriend.

After listening to the developments in Zeynep's life for an hour and a half, I thought I could tell her about my experience with the optician. But Zeynep was already getting ready to leave.

On the way back home, I entered the building by walking from places where the optician couldn't see me. When I entered, I was face to face with the disorganized furniture and unpacked boxes.

I decided to take one of them out. The first package I grabbed was the bed. I took out the wooden pieces inside. When I read the instructions on the package, I realized that I couldn't do this mounting job alone.

But I still wanted to try. I had to do it somehow. I lifted one of them and wedged it between my legs. I tried to attach the other wooden piece to the corner. It was both heavy and difficult. It wasn't easy to keep it balanced because it was long.

After struggling to pass one of the corners, I needed to go to the other corner. However, if I let go of the piece I was holding with my legs, everything would collapse.

I brought the bed parts closer to the wall with small movements. I was trying not to drop the wooden piece I was holding between my legs. I started to sweat. I hadn't opened the window. But now I couldn't because the assembled parts would fall apart as soon as I moved from my place.

When I approached the wall, I tried to lean and fix the two wooden pieces I had connected at the corners. After being sure it stayed balanced for a while, I let go of the wooden piece between my legs. It could stand still for a few seconds.

But then all the parts fell to the ground and scattered. The drops of sweat on my forehead were about to fall into my eyes. I wiped my forehead on my arm in anger and opened the window.

I didn't want to spend the same evening at home. I randomly bought a movie ticket and went to the cinema. In the action movie I watched, the main character was fighting against a criminal organization alone.

When I came home at night, I entered the house without turning on the lights. I didn't want to face the mess in the house. I turned on my computer and encountered the same survey again.

"What is the name of your best friend?"

Only the computer screen and the reflection of the outside light illuminated the living room. I watched the reflections on the wall for a while.

I felt a tightness in my chest.

Under the survey question, I wrote "Myself." I had no one else but myself.

If I had a best friend, it was me.

The next morning, I went to the café on my street for breakfast and a coffee. I had to pick up my weight set from the optician. But I didn't want to remember this stress again. Suppressing my thoughts, I went to the café…".

When it was my turn to order, I asked for a creamy latte. The cashier asked, "Do you mean a caramel latte?"

"No," I said. "I want a single shot latte with cream on top."

"Sir, we don't have such a drink," said the cashier. "I can give you a caramel latte."

Normally, I would say "Okay" and not make a fuss. I didn't want to create a problem and I felt pressured by the people in line behind me. But I was getting annoyed.

Very annoyed.

"You can enter the order as a caramel latte," I said, emphasizing my words. "But please don't put caramel in it. You're just adding a bit of cream to a single shot latte, how hard can that be?"

The cashier looked at the people behind me, took my order, and I went to find a nice table. They would call my name for my order, after all.

I sat down at a table and took out my notebook and hand cream from my bag when a stranger left a drink at my table.

"A single shot latte with cream," the person said. He was young like me.

"I ordered the same after seeing yours," he said. He raised his cardboard cup in a cheers gesture. "I wanted to bring you your drink to say thank you."

"Thank you very much," I said.

"The other creamy drinks are too sweet for me," he continued. "But I like the cream."

"I'm the same way. Sweet drinks are too much," I said. "I'm glad you like the creamy latte."

"But mine isn't a single shot," he said. "A single shot of espresso never does it for me. I have too much work to do."

"Good luck with that," I said. I couldn't understand why he was trying to start a dialogue with me.

After a pause, he asked, "What do you do?"

I had come here to relax and get away from the stress of home. Now I found myself in dialogue with a stranger. All I wanted was to be left alone.

"I moved to a new place this week," I said. "I came to the café to take a break from housework." I hoped they would understand my message.

"Moving is a big problem, really," he said. He then sat down at the table next to me. "I moved six months ago. I came from a different city, so I didn't know anyone here. I had to do everything by myself. It was really hard."

In my head, I said, "Welcome to my life," but all that came out of my mouth was "Yes."

He sipped his creamy latte. Then he looked at me with great excitement and said, "I can help you. If you want, of course. I know how hard moving can be. Your friends and family are probably helping, but the more help you get, the better. Maybe I can lend a hand. What do you say?"

What would you think if a stranger offered to solve your problems? I would be suspicious.

But I needed help. I wanted to say “Yes."

I imagined lifting my weight set from the optician's shop and then unpacking and assembling the furniture in my home. I must have been too absorbed in this daydream because I woke up to the person in front of me speaking again.

"I'm sorry," he said. "I disturbed you. It must seem strange for someone you don't know to suddenly offer these things. I didn't realize how weird it would sound."

"No," I wanted to say. But I could only manage, "It's no problem."

"I managed to sort out everything by asking for help from my neighbor," he said. "They were really nice. We had a few quick chats in the building and got along well. Thanks to them, I was able to move in easily."

Seeing that I didn't respond, he spoke again. "Anyway, I'm still talking. I'm sorry for bothering you. I wish you all the best. Have a good day," he said, getting up and walking away.

I thought about my neighbor in the building. I had bumped into someone who lived on the ground floor a few times and had some quick chats. He seemed a few years older than me.

I thought about going back to my apartment and knocking on my neighbor's door. But I remembered the stressful moments I had with the optician. It had seemed like a logical decision at the time. But I thought about the possibility that he might take a bad attitude towards me or if he couldn't say no and he came anyway, we might experience strange moments.

I froze with my hand on the doorbell. I remembered the day I met him. He was friendly and very sweet. "I can take this risk," I thought.

When he opened the door, he greeted me with the same friendly smile. I asked if he could help me carry the weight set up from the optician. That was the biggest problem I wanted to solve. Because the optician had caused me extreme stress for the past two days.

My neighbor accepted without hesitation and put on their slippers to come down with me. We carried the weight set up from the optician together.

After we put the set inside, I was about to thank them when he saw the furniture and unpacked boxes inside.

"I can also help with assembly if you want," he said.

With a little hesitation, I managed to say, "Yes." We had unpacked and assembled almost everything by the end of the day. Bed, wardrobe, bedside table, TV unit, coffee table, dining table...

"You have such beautiful things. You will have a very sweet home," he said. My heart warmed.

By the end of the day, I realized I was hungry and offered to order food for my neighbor. When I opened my computer, I completely forgot I would see the survey.

The same question was staring back at me.

"What is your best friend's name?"

I opened another tab and began to evaluate the options to be able to order food.

"Were you doing that survey too?" he asked.

"Just before you rang the bell, I was also doing this survey. How interesting, isn't it? There were some really weird questions. And the answer to the question about what kind of friend you are can't be an emotional support friend or activity partner.”

"Yes, it seemed very strange to me too," I said.

"It was a very judgmental survey. But it was also quite profound, actually."

"What do you mean?" I asked.

"For example, there was one very good question. The question asks for the name of your closest friend. At first, I didn't understand how writing any name there would change anything for me. But after thinking about it a little, I realized that it was testing whether I could provide an answer or not," he said.

I nodded my head indicating that I was listening to him while continuing to choose the food.

"Actually, it's not asking for your best friend. It's testing whether you allow anyone to be your closest friend. Are you open or closed to people, in other words. That's why it was a very interesting survey," he said.

What he said made me think a lot. Even though I hadn't given any answers, I started to analyze myself internally. Was I too closed off to the outside world?

"People who want to be your closest friend are out there somewhere. The important thing is whether you allow them or not," he said.

He didn't expect an answer from me. This relaxed me because my brain hadn't processed what I had heard yet and I hadn't understood how these learnings were affecting my emotions.

I told him that I had ordered food. After a while, our food arrived, and we ate it together with a very nice conversation before he returned to his apartment. He had said he would come to help me again the next day.

After he left, I opened the survey again. I came face to face with the same question.

"What is the name of your closest friend?"

I had written "Myself" below it, but I deleted it.

I began to think about who would want to be my closest friend. Various names came to mind. I realized how I had closed myself off to all of them.

I remembered that I hadn't told them anything. I thought I had always chosen the wrong friendships. But even with friends who genuinely wanted to hear about what was going on in my life, I was giving evasive answers.

Perhaps I was the reason for my loneliness.
