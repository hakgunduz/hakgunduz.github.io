title: "Eavesdropper"
date: 2023-03-12 16:30:35 +0000
---

I haven't been able to sleep for three days, I thought as I played with the necklace in my pocket. I had stolen Serpil's necklace from the jeweler just three days ago.

While I had not been able to sleep for three days, my relationship with wakefulness was gradually broken. With each passing hour, I was becoming more and more sleepy, as if I was the sleep itself. Noticing that I was slouching again, I straightened my back.

My eyes had been twitching for a while. On top of that, on the beach I came to, the sun was reflecting off the sea and hitting my eyes, causing them to twitch even more. Why did I come here? On this beautiful day, I didn't deserve the sunshine. I was polluting the peaceful air of the beach.

This feeling was familiar to me now. I felt as if the sun was reflecting on my eyes from Serpil's eyes. I hadn't been able to look at her since the day she sold her grandmother's necklace to the jeweler for my master's degree.

The sun, both direct and reflected from the sea, was burning my skin. I forgot to apply sunscreen. Deep down, I thought I actually didn’t put it on on purpose.

When Serpil put something in her mind, no one could make her give up on her decision. I told her many times that if I could freeze the master's degree, I would freeze it, and if I couldn't, I wouldn't go. But she didn't want me to lose this chance to go abroad.

“This is a life-saving opportunity, you cannot miss it,” she was saying. I told her not to sell the necklace, and to wait. I thought I'd be able to convince her as time went on.

But one day she showed up with the money I needed for my master's degree. I took her to the cafe where we had the first discussion about where our problems started. After she gave me this news there, she immediately transferred the money to my account. I wished we had met in another place that day.

I visualized how I would get home. On the way, there was both a jewelry store and a cafe where I got this news. I made a note in my mind by determining a route to go home without seeing both of them.

One day, I told Serpil why my family could not support me. My father's hospital expenses had already put them in enough debt, and they would need more. I could never forgive myself for not being able to help them in this process. There was nothing I could do when my father was getting weaker before my eyes.

This story probably had an impact on Serpil's decision. I wish I had never told her that story.

While squeezing the necklace in my pocket tightly with my fingers, I thought of texting Serpil. When the money she transferred to my account that day was added to our arguments that had been going on for a while, we started to talk less.

“Did she want to buy me to get rid of me,” I thought for the first time. Was I that terrible? But would she sell her grandmother's necklace to do that? Maybe I was that terrible.

The thought of doing a master's degree abroad seemed so distant at the time that I was sure it wouldn't happen. But it would be an insult to Serpil if it didn't happen. I couldn't do this to her.

When I learned what Serpil did, my first thought was to go and make a deal with the jeweler. I found out which jeweler she sold the necklace to. I went to talk with them.

“If you can wait about nine or ten months, I might buy the necklace back,” I said.

“Unfortunately, I can't wait that long, there are people who are already interested,” the jeweler said.

I told him how precious the necklace was to me. My hands and feet were shaking as I spoke, but I was tense to not make a wrong move or say anything wrong. No matter what I said, the jeweler was not convinced.

Not sure how I clenched my fist because my fingers had been sore ever since. The last point was that the jeweler tried to sell it back to me for almost double the price.

As I squinted my eyes against the sun reflecting off the sea, I pressed the necklace in my pocket to my leg and felt the pain on my skin.

The thought of stealing the necklace occurred to me that day. However, a few days later, I remembered what the jeweler said. I went to bargain again.

“I'll buy it back in nine months at twenty percent interest,” I said.

“Seventy percent,” the jeweler said. "And how do I know you won't disappear?"

"I'm not going to disappear," I said. "Forty percent."

"Fifty percent."

"Agreed."

I gave him all my contact information. I wrote down where I lived, where I would live in Europe, and which department I would study, and left them to the jeweler. But I still couldn't trust him. The thought of him selling the necklace to someone else was disturbing my nights.

I had already started the plans to steal the necklace when the scenarios in my mind had become unbearable.

I broke my friend Necmi's phone the day before the robbery to testify that I was with him at the time of the robbery. He sent it for repair. I set his computer's clock to another location's clock. I also matched my own phone's time to it.

I bought sleeping pills from the pharmacy and put them in my pocket. That same night, I waited two blocks from the jeweler's until no one was left on the street. Then I walked towards the house, and when I was just one street away from the house, I threw myself into the darkness of the cul-de-sac I always passed and changed my clothes. I wore all black and put on my ski mask.

When I arrived in front of the jewelry store, all I had to do was melt the wires of the alarm. I learned how to do this by looking up on the internet for days. Then I walked around the back street, through an apartment building, and reached the back garden. From here, the jeweler's back window could be accessed.

I removed the points where the iron bars meet the wall. Then I cut the window glass. When I walked in I realized that I had forgotten about something very critical. If I just stole the necklace, it would be obvious that I was the thief.

Whether I had a witness or not, that would have created a lot of problems. So I had to steal not only the necklace but also many other jewels besides the necklace. I stuffed my pockets and quickly ran away.

When I arrived at Necmi’s place, the clock in the house and on my phone was three hours behind. We spent the whole evening together and watched movies. Necmi still couldn't seem sleepy when my wrong clock showed six. However, if he stayed a little longer, he might begin to question why there’s no sunrise.

That's why I put sleeping pills in his coke. Within ten minutes he was gone. I left Necmi's house and went to my own house. When I spilled the jewels in my pocket on the table, I realized that tears came to my eyes.

I fell to my knees and started sobbing.

I think I bled my leg while pressing the necklace in my pocket. I felt a sense of warmth. The sun hitting my eyes no longer bothered me.

At that moment, I heard two people talking in front of me.

“They found the thief of the jewelry store robbery the other day,” said the red beret walking ahead. The expression on the guy with a green jacket next to him was filled with question marks.

I decided to get up from the bench where I had been sitting for hours and follow these two. I was listening to what they were saying. The colors of the red beret and green jacket were very similar to the colors of Serpil's necklace. At that moment, I realized what I was thinking about and got angry.

“He calls himself a lock picker, a thief who claims to open any lock. This was the eighth place he robbed,” red beret continued.

“As if he were playing basketball, he would rip out the locks he opened and throw them in a nearby trash. According to what the police have explained, a person who does this for sports, not because he needs it, I thought. It's like he's trying to show off."

“But why?” said the green jacket. I was walking a few steps behind the two of them, pretending to examine my surroundings.

“Who knows, maybe he's trying to prove something. They've actually come close to arresting him a few times, but he escaped jail time, either with a lawyer or with his father's money.”

“So they suspect that this thief broke into the jewelry store I was in,” I thought.

“I think the police gave up on the case a bit when they realized that the thief was obvious but there wasn't enough evidence to arrest him,” the red beret said. "That's why they made the announcement as if they wanted to warn people that this thief might break in somewhere else."

It occurred to me that I should go to the jeweler as if I didn't know anything and ask if the necklace was there. This was a step that was included in my heist plan. A few days after the robbery, I would go to the jewelry store and pretend to hear from them for the first time. Then for a few weeks, I would ask if the necklace had been found.

I had to prepare myself for police interrogation. But when I heard about this other thief, I thought maybe it wouldn't even be necessary. I was ready to feel relieved, but I realized that I was angry.

“A thief who steals for sport and he gets away with it,” I thought. Except for Serpil's necklace, I wanted to donate the jewels I stole to the jeweler. It wasn't clear in my mind how I can do this. But I would find a way to pay back all the money I stole.

“Did the jeweler have insurance?” asked the green jacket.

“Of course, the equivalent of all the stolen jewels was paid by the insurance company,” red beret said.

“So the money that insurance companies received from other companies covered the amount stolen here. Many industries helped to pay the money that is now in the thief's pocket,” said the green jacket.

“Actually, we paid for it indirectly,” red beret said. “We are the customers of those companies, after all.”

“Ultimately, the consumer pays for everything.”

Thinking about what I've heard, I've got all the jewels, I thought. My first thought was to bury the jewels somewhere. Then the thought occurred to me that I could donate them.

The next day I went to the jeweler and kept asking about the necklace. It was as I expected. I never had to deal with the cops. Everything was very normal in the jewelry store. It was as if there had been no theft. They were still bothered by my asking about the necklace. I was creating unnecessary headaches for them.

I could not give the necklace to Serpil. I had planned to wait at least a year for this. I told her that the necklace from the jewelry store was not stolen and that I would repurchase it in a year. She had already forgotten the necklace. But I wasn't going to let this one go.

At our last meeting, I felt that all ties between us were almost broken. After saying goodbye to her, I went to sell one of the jewels to make my first payment for my master's degree. I found another jeweler at the other end of the city and set off.

I thought about the moment I sat on the beach bench. Again, I felt my helplessness against the sun that scorched my skin and my eyes. I was so weak against the sun that I couldn't do anything.

I could not cover the sea so that the light reflected from the sea would not catch my eyes. I couldn't shut down the sky so it wouldn't burn my skin.

I realized that all I could do was let the sun change me. I felt defeated like never before in my life.
