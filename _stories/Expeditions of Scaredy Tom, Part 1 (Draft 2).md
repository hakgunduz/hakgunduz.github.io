When he entered the circle, Tom thought there would be no one. The refined forest seemed quiet that day. But an old man was sitting on top of a ginormous root. Its height was almost the size of two regular-sized people.

As a regular-sized thirteen years old, Tom averted his gaze not acknowledging the old man. He walked inside the circle shoulders hunched and looking down, hoping his clear-cut bangs hid his blushed face.

The circle was a small gathering of short trees of different kinds and various bushes among the ancient roots; one of which the old man sat on.

The ancient roots were connected with the tall trees covering the sky. The tall trees formed a forest that they called the ‘refined forest’. The giant roots were surrounding the short trees as if to protect them, forming a complicated maze.

The rays of sunlight were striking the short trees. They were situated almost as a circle to get enough sun for each; hence the name ‘circle’.

Tom looked like he was a small rat compared to the tall trees. But he was hesitant to look up. He examined the circle and to his surprise, there were many glowing orbs with different hues on the branches of short trees where the sunlight hit.

There were as many hues of blue as it was purple. Tom narrowed his eyes to pick one of the blue hues and headed towards it.

“Don’t you think,” the old man said, “they might be asking for the other ones?” He was wearing shades of blue and grey just like little Tom.

Without making eye contact, Tom said “They asked for the blue one.” But he stopped in his tracks, confused. For a moment all the glows seemed similar to him.

“They asked for the blue bask,” he said.

The old man cracked a burst of ugly laughter that startled little Tom.

“It’s called ‘blue husk,’” he said. “I wish there was a way to test which is which.”

Tom caught a glance of the old man’s smirk.

“They just said I should bring the blue one,” he repeated feeling heat building up on his face. He felt the old man’s gaze on him as he walked back and forth among the orbs. They were the size of a tennis ball which would fit one’s palm perfectly.

Blue hues were just blue. He was certain of it. The orbs the old man mentioned looked more purple to him.

“I guess it’s important for you to bring the right one,” the old man continued. He seemed he was enjoying this.

“I guess you don’t know how to use them yet. How old are you?” The old man asked.

“Thirteen,” Tom answered.

“And you’re still picking blue husk orbs for the tribe?” The old man said, scratching his beard.

“You’re not so bright, aren’t you?” He continued.

Tom rushed to one of the blue orbs and struggled to pluck one. Until finally the branch gave throwing him a few steps behind.

The old man snorted.

“Don’t even know how to pick an orb…”

Little Tom got up and put the blue orb inside his small straw bag which only one could fit. He bolted outside the circle.

Tom walked among the trees not thinking of what happened, trying not to feel anything because of what happened. He walked without taking a break. Until he saw the clearing above their town, the patch of sky.

Their patch of sky. He missed it.

Their town was built in a clearing of tall trees. There were big wooden walls surrounding the settlement and a giant gate.

Tom saw a rough-looking guy on top of the wall next to the entrance gate. He waved his hand. The rough-looking guy disappeared and a yellow glow rose from where he had been.

The earth trembled under little Tom’s feet and the gate started to open.
