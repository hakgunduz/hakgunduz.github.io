---
title: "Hooded Crow"
date: 2023-03-19 16:57:03 +0000
---

"I wonder what the raven we saw today was thinking?" asked the hooded crow. The other crows on the walnut tree looked at him blankly. They couldn't understand what he was saying.

"What a silly question," cackled one of the other crows. The other crows on the walnut tree started to caw as if approving what was being said. All the crows in the circle had heard the question.

The hooded crow felt something he had never felt before: shame. However, he found himself examining the novelty of this feeling, rather than being crushed by it.

The hooded crow was closer to the outer circles of the crow circle that gathered on the walnut tree every evening. He had tried to advance for weeks but had only been able to move a few circles inward.

A voice inside him said that this new question would cost him a few circles. Therefore, he did not want to pursue the subject.

After spending some time on the walnut tree, as he did every day, he returned to the tree where his nest was located. As he began to caw, he questioned why he was cawing. He imagined whether his voice was strong enough and evaluated the strength of the others' voices. Therefore, he could not caw as long as he did every night.

As he returned to his nest, he remembered that a few parts of his nest were made of human hair. A new feeling arose within him: disgust.

The next day, he woke up early to search for food. At the beginning of the street, they went with the group every morning, they met at an electricity pole. This street was full of kebab restaurants, buffets, and various restaurants and cafes. Therefore, the crows could find quite a bit of food.

Every morning, their first target was a young mouse, various insects, or similar small animal carcasses they could eat. However, the hooded crow did not feel like he could catch and kill a live animal that day.

He stole a package of peanuts from the nut stand in front of the nearest buffet and flew to the roof of a building. He was aware that he was going outside of his morning routine. Therefore, he felt the other crows' eyes on him.

After pecking through the package of peanuts and satisfying his hunger, he watched the other crows' behavior from the roof of the building. They all had priorities, which were based on their hunger levels, and the crows took action as a result of these various dynamics.

It was strange to the hooded crow that he had never observed all of this before. Everything that was happening was clear before his eyes. He was watching with interest.

After everyone in the group had satisfied their hunger, they flew to a more wooded area together. They found a tree for themselves and began to clean their feathers with their beaks. They took turns cawing to make sure no other bird would attack them.

When it was the hooded crow's turn to caw, all the crows fell silent. They listened carefully to the absence of sound, trying to understand what was happening.

Meanwhile, the hooded crow had directed all his attention to another tree nearby. He was daydreaming about the events of the previous day.

Here, a solitary raven perched on a branch of the same tree, watching the crows. When one of the other crows came to check up on  the hooded crow, he began to caw in his place. The crow expressed his anger for making them worried by hitting the hooded crow with his wings a few times.

After spending some time, they stopped by the street where they found food and satisfied their hunger. Then they returned to the walnut tree where they roosted every afternoon. There was no change in the circles that day.

The hooded crow was once again under the influence of a new feeling: relief. He took a deep breath.

When he started cleaning his feathers with his beak, he realized that he had not combed his feathers in the order he did every day. The feeling he had previously felt had returned. He had recognized the feeling of ‘shame’ this time. His eyes secretly watched the other crows to see if they were looking at him.

After making sure that the other crows were not watching him, he quickly cleaned and straightened his feathers. Meanwhile, a series of voices emanating from the inner circles of the walnut tree echoed to the outermost circle, commanding the hooded crow to return to the outermost circle.

The hooded crow returned to the outermost circle of the walnut tree without resistance. He was still hungry. He looked at the walnuts that the other crows had thrown on the street. Some of them had even been run over by cars, but no one seemed to be moving to pick them up.

Normally, he would fly down and snack on the walnuts. However, he thought that if he did so, the other crows would unite and chase him down and not allow him to eat the walnuts.

He returned to his nest without satisfying his hunger. Although he remembered that he had to sing before entering his nest that day, he felt something preventing him from doing so. He thought that if he started to sing, the other crows might recognize his voice and come here.

He thought they would notice the weakness and indecisiveness in his voice. He tried to remember how he used to sing, but no memory came to his mind.

When he returned to his nest, he saw human hair again. He removed all of it from the walls of the nest with his beak. He would find new pieces of branches to replace them, but at that moment, the female crow returned to the nest and saw what the hooded crow was doing, and attacked him.

Within a few seconds, the hooded crow was expelled from the nest.

He thought of returning to the walnut tree but remembered that it was a matter of time before he was expelled from there as well. He flew to one of his old nests. He landed on the upper branches of the tree and tried to see the nest. When he saw the owl inside the nest through the branches, fear engulfed his entire body. For a moment, he couldn't move, but then he slowly walked towards the end of the branch he was perched on and started flying towards another place.

While flying, he glided with the fear that the owl might come from behind and catch him at any moment. Although some crows might hear and notice the owl's flight, the hooded crow could never hear it. He did not trust himself in this regard.

A sequence came to his mind, he visualized it vividly and made a list of other crows that heard such things with their senses. He put these crows in order according to their abilities, and he was at the bottom of this list.

While soaring through the air, he noticed the city lights. He had never looked at this harmonious light dance in this way before. The intense feelings within him suddenly left their negative effect on his body and were replaced by a positive sensation.

He had lost everything in a single day, and he still couldn't understand why. After soaring over the city alone for hours, he landed on a tree branch, tired. A few seconds later, he realized that this was the branch where he had seen the raven.

"What was the raven thinking that day?"

He put himself in the raven's place in his mind. The lifestyle he had learned until then had suddenly shifted, giving way to endless possibilities.

"How would someone else, as a different species, live?"

For the first time in his life, he wondered such a question. He could not name this new feeling. It seemed like too many things were mixed up inside him, and he struggled to distinguish and categorize his emotions.

Just then, a starling landed on one of the trees in front of him. Shortly after, several starlings covered the tree, perching on the branches close to each other.

The hooded crow noticed that the starling group did not have a circle structure similar to their group. None of them stayed in one place, constantly moving around.

After a while, the starlings started to fly. They began to dance together in the illuminated sky of the city, hovering together and creating a choreography that changed shape as a group.

The hooded crow watched this dance all night. As the dance continued, the confusion of his emotions calmed down and gave way to one thing:

Peace.

Then he realized that another feeling had awakened within him:

Inspiration.

The next day, he followed the restaurant worker who was taking out the garbage and directed him to throw the food scraps into a plastic bucket placed next to the trash instead of inside it. All the other crows gathered there and satisfied their hunger.

After the meal, he raised his gray feathers that he had been trying to hide until that day with his beak. When other crows began to clean their feathers with their beaks on the surrounding trees, the hooded crow let himself fall from the branch he was perching on.

He fell towards the ground for a long time without flapping his wings, but just as he was about to hit the ground, he flapped his wings and quickly rose. The other crows were watching this show. Then the hooded crow started to draw circles in the sky above the trees.

He could feel the wind slipping under his wings and wanted to rise higher and higher.

In the late afternoon, he joined the crow circle at the center of the walnut tree without paying attention to the circle system. While perched on the thin branch, the hooded crow began to flap his wings.

All the walnuts on the branch fell onto the street below. However, the hooded crow had not yet flown away. After breaking off the branch, he headed towards one of the highest points of the walnut tree and began to build a nest.

Some crows started telling the other crows in the inner circle what the hooded crow had done that day. The oldest crows in the group called the hooded crow over.

The other crows were watching with great attention what was happening. They hadn't made a sound for a while and had started to become afraid that an owl or a hawk might approach their area.

The patience of the older crows who were trying to get the hooded crow to listen to them began to wear thin. Just then, the hooded crow spread his wings and began to fly. Just like he did at noon that day, he glided over the top of the walnut tree and started to dance.

The older crows followed him and, while trying to catch the hooded crow, they accidentally glided along with him and began to accompany the dance.

The crows below began to sing together.

It was as if the hooded crow and the older crows were dancing in the sky and they were all keeping a rhythm together.
