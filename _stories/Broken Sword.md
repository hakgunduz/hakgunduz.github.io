title: "Broken Sword"
date: 2023-04-09 18:31:57 +0000
---

"He was the only warrior who didn't have his sword made by Angus," he said. During the feast, someone pulled his friend aside into a corner. "Only his sword was broken, Angus's swords were still intact."

"Some say it's because of Jin. According to rumors, he killed more people than anyone else," said the other person.

The village's warriors had returned after a year-long war, and Jin was among them. A large table was set up in the center of the village in their honor, and a feast was underway.

At one end of the table, Angus sat without taking off his coat, which hung over his imposing shoulders. The surrounding warriors were offering him refreshments, constantly raising their glasses to Angus's name.

At the other end of the table, the village elders surrounded Jin. He was a tall, long-haired warrior, and his facial features matched his height. Due to his large, characteristic nose, he often had to remove his protective helmet on the battlefield.

"To our warriors' honor!" said one of the elders, raising his glass.

"To the honor of our best warrior, Jin," said another.

Young men were running around and trying to steal drinks secretly. Two of them reached for a drink at the same time, and the bigger one pushed the other to the ground. He grabbed the drink and proudly looked at his fallen rival.

Jin had watched what had happened. He didn't want to listen to the elders. However, he couldn't leave the feast.

Another young man and his friend had stolen a bottle of drink from the table. They ran to a nearby street and began to fill stolen cups with beer.

"I heard Hennes killed 18 people. That was the most I heard," said one of the young men.

"I wonder how many people Jin killed?" asked the other.

"I wish they had taken us with them."

"I'll be old enough next year. I'll go with Jin!"

Angus had become bored with the young warriors around him. He asked for permission to leave. He said he would start making a new sword the next day and left them.

"How disciplined he is," said one of the young warriors.

"That's why his swords are the best!"

"Then learn something from Angus's discipline," said the older warrior who had just joined them. The others changed their stance immediately when they heard his voice.

"Bring everyone another bottle," he shouted to one of the servants. The young warriors shouted excitedly and raised their glasses again.

They dragged someone in front of Jin. Voices rose from the crowd, "This sword master is a disgrace to the village."

"I pushed the sword too hard. I broke it," said Jin.

Another voice rose from the crowd, "The swords he makes can't stand up to your strength."

Many people agreed with this statement.

"Get a sword made by Angus," said one of the elders.

"Grandfather," said Jin.

Jin's grandfather approached him. "The sword is an honor. The sword is your life. The lives of your loved ones and fellow warriors are entrusted to your sword," he said.

"Angus is a good sword maker," he continued. "The first thing you should do tomorrow is to go and have him make you a sword."

Jin didn't like what he heard, but he didn't respond to his grandfather.

After his grandfather finished speaking, he signaled to the young girls waiting in a corner. The girls came over to Jin. One of them sat on his lap.

Jin looked for his wife. He saw her sitting with his other wives on the right side of the table. She smiled at him.

The next day, Jin woke early and headed to Angus's sword shop. There was a tradition in their village that a warrior had to watch the entire process of his sword being made.

When he arrived at the shop, he watched Angus for a while without making any noise.

Angus pulled a block of metal out of the hot coals. He headed to the forging machine and hammered the metal until it became thin and long. The metal had begun to take the shape of a sword when Angus quickly plunged it into the water.

Afterward, he broke the metal into several pieces with a hammer and arranged the broken pieces on top of each other, wrapping them in thick cardboard-like material. Then, he took a piece of mud from a small bucket next to him and spread it over the cardboard.

He then wrapped the cardboard with a type of metal wire and went to the stove to stoke the coals for more heat. He pushed a wooden stick mounted on the wall back and forth to heat the coals more. When the coals were hot enough, he pushed the metal pieces he had prepared into the hot coals.

When he wiped his forehead and turned around, he saw Jin. They looked into each other's eyes. After a moment of silence, Angus turned his head and continued working.

"I need a sword," said Jin.

Angus remained silent for a while longer.

"You never used to come to me," he said.

"I didn't want to come," said Jin.

"Your grandfather has become obsessed with my swords," he said proudly. "He's been telling people for days that there were no swords of this quality in his time."

"I'm sure he's exaggerating," said Jin.

"But you're still here," Angus replied.

"My sword master's job was taken away from him. He won't be able to make swords anymore."

"Shame," said Angus. "I trained him too."

"You're all the same in my eyes," said Jin. "Will you make me a sword or not?"

"Do you warriors think you're so different?"

"Those who talk the most about war are those who never go to war," Jin said bluntly.

Angus acted like he hadn't heard this.

"A group of proud fools who don't know what they're fighting for."

"How would you know what it's like? You've never been on a battlefield once in your life," Jin said.

"But still, my swords have taken hundreds of lives..."

"One of them was my brother," Jin breathed. His tone of voice changed.

Angus extracted the metal from the coal and went to the forging machine to continue hammering the metal for a while.

As the machine hit the metal, lights were scattered around.

"Do you want to take another life?" asked Jin. "I'm waiting for my sword."

Angus said nothing. He pulled the metal from under the forging machine, took a hammer in his hand, and hammered the metal with all his might.

Jin listened to the sound of the hammer hitting the metal for a while.

After Angus stopped hammering the metal, he raised his head.

"What made me such a good swordsmith was the sword I made for your brother," he said.

"The mistake you made cost my brother his life," Jin shouted.

"If the handle of his sword hadn't come loose, he would have won the duel. I watched my brother die with my own eyes!"

Jin drew his sword. He seemed to want to attack Angus. But he was holding himself back.

In response, Angus also pulled the iron from the hot coals. Flaming particles spread around.

"Will you make me a sword or not?" Jin yelled. Tears filled his eyes.

Angus saw the broken point of Jin's sword. He examined the marks of the strikes that continued from end to end. There were notches in various parts of the sword.

He noticed the cut marks on Jin's face for the first time. The cut marks continued on his arms. He felt as if there were these marks all over his body.

Jin seemed to be in pain. He was holding one shoulder slightly down.

Angus saw the bandage mark under his shirt.

"I wish I had never made that sword for your brother," Angus said.

"I wish I had never made any swords... I wish I hadn't gone into your uncle's workshop as a child, thinking it was a playground."

"Would it have been better to go to war with us?" Jin asked. "Is it better to have to take lives?"

Angus put the metal he had been hammering back into the hot coals. Then, he pulled it out. As he continued to hit it with the hammer, he started talking.

"I had hammered your brother's sword too much. It was an amateur mistake. The only problem wasn't in the handle. That sword was about to break in the heat of battle."

Jin threw his broken sword to the ground.

"I learned with that sword. If you hammer the metal too much, it becomes weaker instead of stronger," Angus continued.

"Your brother, your uncle, my father... They were swords that had been hammered too much. You, on the other hand, are a masterpiece. Use your sword to fight or protect your loved ones. At the end of the day, you will continue to take lives. And I will continue to make weapons that take those lives..."

The metal he was hitting with the hammer had taken the shape of a sword. Jin listened to the sound of the hammer for a while.

Angus's courtyard was surrounded by various plants. The garden had an elegance that was in contrast to the roughness of the sword workshop. Various colored flowers had bloomed among the plants.

He saw a bee. It was flying over a red flower.

Suddenly, a sound of water rang in his ears. When he looked towards the corner of the garden, he saw a small fountain. Water was flowing. He had not heard the sound of water until that moment.

The water continued to flow, following the path prepared for watering the plants.

Jin took a deep breath.

"My brother killed a child on the first day of the war," he said.

The sound of the hammer suddenly stopped.

"Since that day, he could never be the same. In the following months, his behavior changed along with the course of the war. It was as if he wanted to die."

Angus did not move. He listened to him with his back turned.

"One night, I saw him playing with the handle of his sword," Jin continued.

"The next day, after a fight he was involved in, he invited one of our own warriors to a duel. During that duel, the handle of his sword came off. Immediately after, his opponent thrust his sword down from his neck."

Angus put the metal back into the hot coals.

"We couldn't get the sword out of his shoulder for a few minutes," Jin said.

"There was a pool of blood. All my clothes were stained with my brother's blood. My hands were slipping as I tried to remove the sword."

"After a few attempts, we had to stop and catch our breath. We were looking at my brother's corpse. We tried to remove the sword with the person who killed him."

Angus immediately sat on a nearby stool, trying to hide his tears.

"I remembered that sword. It was the first sword you and my uncle forged…"
