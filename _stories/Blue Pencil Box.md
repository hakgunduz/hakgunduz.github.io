---
title: "Blue Pencil Box"
date: 2023-04-02 19:07:35 +0000
---

When Sema noticed the blue pen box she had seen in her dream on someone else's desk, she was sitting at the corner table where she always sat.

This café she frequented was located at a four-way intersection, the liveliest spot in her neighborhood. Both walls facing the street were covered with windows, making it a perfect place to watch people pass by.

However, no matter how much Sema envied the tables by the windows, she always sat in the same spot. The table she sat at was against the wall, behind a large worktable and bar stools.

She chose this spot although it provided the least access to the view of the street.

That was how she noticed the blue pen box. While admiring the tables by the windows, she suddenly noticed the blue color on the table. It was exactly the color she had seen in her dream, and the shape was also the same.

When she looked at the owner of the pen box, she saw a young woman with long, messy but stylishly tied brown hair, writing something in her notebook. She appeared to be the same age as Sema, but very different from her.

The woman sitting by the window seemed to be free and was drawing while watching the street, without hiding anything from anyone.

Sema felt strange when she thought about this. The person in front of her was not like her at all.

She kept watching her until her friend Naif came.

"Sorry I'm late," Naif said as she sat beside Sema. “Why did you sit here again?”

He was about to complain when he realized Sema's attention was elsewhere.

Looking secretly at the girl by the window, he made a gesture as if to ask what was going on.

"The blue pen box on the table," said Sema. "I saw it in my dream.”

"Did you see the girl in your dream too?”

"No, I only saw the blue pen box," Sema replied.

"Anyway, there was someone who owned the pen box in your dream, and there is someone who owns it in real life," Naif said.

These words gave Sema an idea.

"I have to go back to my dream and make sure," she said.

"Go back to your dream?” Naif began to speak, but Sema had already packed her things and left the café in a hurry.

As she left, she didn't turn her head toward the girl by the window. However, the girl noticed her anyway. After Sema left, the girl by the window looked toward Naif, but Naif looked away.

The next day, as Sema was getting ready for work, she noticed the shiny maroon sweater that had been sitting in her wardrobe since the day she bought it. After examining it for a while, she put on a black shirt and a black sweater instead and stood in front of the mirror.

After applying light makeup that was barely noticeable, she turned her attention to her lipsticks. She chose the one that was closest to her lip color and applied it. Although, she could have just used some lip gloss because the lipstick didn’t stand out more because it was matte and almost hid her lips.

She had to go to the office that day. After a long and varied series of meetings, she was about to end the day with a long bus ride. However, she remembered at the last minute that she had to go to the charity she volunteered for. She was helping autistic children and playing games with them.

When she entered the charity's workspace, three of the children ran up to her and hugged her legs, shouting, "Sema!"

The instructor came over to Sema and said, "They love you so much, they've been calling your name all day."

"Anyone would do the same," Sema said. "They love the person who plays with them. That's why they've been calling my name all day because Sema means playing," she said, laughing as if she had made a joke.

The instructor did not respond to Sema's comment.

When she arrived home, she was quite tired, and it was past nine o'clock. She had forgotten to eat dinner. She had a few leftovers in the fridge, so she had her dinner. She had no appetite.

While getting ready for bed she thought of her blue pencil case. She thought of it until she fell asleep. But when she woke up in the morning, she couldn't remember any of her dreams.

When she forced herself a little, she remembered a moment when pages filled with various scribbles and drawings were floating in the air. She couldn't remember if she had made it up or if she had seen it in her dream.

The next morning, she began by examining the maroon sweater in her wardrobe again. After not wearing it and putting on a black outfit instead, she went to the same café to work.

When she entered the cafe, the table by the window was empty. However, she headed to the table she always sat at in the back. She opened her computer and started working while keeping an eye on the table by the window. She was curious whether the girl would come to the cafe again or not.

After a while, a young couple sat at the table. Sema decided to focus on her work, thinking that she wouldn't see that girl again. After working all day, she went to the charity where she volunteered.

She skipped dinner again and fell asleep thinking about her blue pencil box. She was sure that the floating drawings she had seen in her dream were there. However, she couldn't see the blue pencil box in her dream.

The next day, she spent her day the same way, working in the cafe and going to the association afterward. She still couldn't see the blue pencil box in her dream at night.

She thought about what would happen if she saw that the blue pencil box belonged to the same girl in her dream. She didn't know why she wanted to see her dream again.

She reminded herself that she could talk to her in her dream.

In her dream, the cafe could take any shape she wanted. The table she always sat at turned into the most beautiful table in the cafe. She could see the whole way from where she was sitting. She didn't have to envy the tables by the window anymore.

In her dream, she didn't hesitate to take out her notebook and pencil box from her bag. She knew that when the girl by the window looked at her, she wouldn't see Sema. In Sema's mind, her dreams and reality were in different dimensions. She was an external viewer in her dreams.

Suddenly, she found herself playing with the children at the charity. That day, the parents had come to join the games too. One of the parents, an old woman, approached Sema.

"What's bothering you, my child?" she asked.

Sema was very surprised by this question, was stunned, and didn't know what to say.

"No, mam. Nothing's bothering me," she managed to say.

"Tell me what's on your mind," the woman insisted.

Sema suddenly felt very tired. She couldn't carry the walls she always carried. What would happen if she acted as if she was against this old lady?

"I was thinking about a dream I saw a few days ago. I want to see it again," she said.

"It's a matter of the heart," said the woman.

"It's not a matter of the heart," Sema interrupted, but the woman cut her off.

"You have to do everything exactly as you did on the day you had the dream to see the same dream again," she said.

What she heard stressed Sema more than it relieved her. She smiled at the old lady with an anxious expression. "Of course, mom, I'll try. Thank you very much," she said.

When she saw the blue pen box in her dream, she went to a small theater show with Naif's insistence. While the actors continued the play, they sometimes threw remarks at the audience and asked questions.

Sema, throughout the show, listened to her heartbeat instead of watching the show with the fear that they would throw remarks at her also.

Then, again, they went to a place with a few friends they coincidentally met at the theater show, thanks to Naif's insistence. She needed Naif's help to be able to repeat this day, but she never liked to ask for help.

She felt even more miserable. The thought that she could not see the blue pen box again in her dream saddened her.

In the following days, she devoted herself more to work. She visited the café less frequently. She skipped meals.

She realized that she had not cleaned her house for a few weeks. After seeing the dust on her desk, she went to the café to work.

After sitting in the café for a while, Naif suddenly entered with his work bag. They looked at each other and smiled.

As Naif approached Sema, he turned his attention to the woman sitting at the large desk and her colorful cardigan.

"How beautiful it looks on you," he said.

The woman looked at Naif in surprise. Then her face was covered with a warm smile. "Really?" she said.

"I wasn't sure when I wore it. Thank you very much. You made my day," she said.

Then both Naif and the woman went back to their work as if nothing had happened. Sema watched everything with great surprise. She looked at the woman in admiration.

That night, Sema saw her maroon sweater in her dream. But when she woke up, she could not feel the inspiration she felt in her dream. She had promised herself in her dream that she would wear her maroon sweater when she woke up.

When she saw the sweater in her wardrobe, she hesitated again. She wore a black sweater instead. She felt a weight inside her. It was as if a fog had covered all her internal organs.

She went back to the café. As she walked, all the troubles she had experienced until that day filled her inside. She couldn't shake off the feeling of every day being the same. She thought of the repeating gray days and the tedious routine.

When she arrived at the café, she noticed that the sun was shining on the table by the other window, not where she saw the blue pen box. She thought she should take the step she could not take for her maroon sweater here.

Although her whole body started physically warning her, she sat at the table by the window. She felt like her heart would jump out of her chest. She realized she wasn't breathing.

She wanted to take out her notebook and pens from her bag. She wanted to act as she always did, but she felt like everyone in the café was watching her.

Just then, the girl with the blue pen box entered. Then she sat at the table by the other window.

Sema remembered another thing she had seen in her dream at that moment. The drawings she had seen floating in the air before were on the table. There was a feeling inside her that the blue pen box was also very close, but she couldn't see the box.

A voice said, "Your drawings are so beautiful."

Another voice said, "No, they're nothing. I just scribble them."

This was the voice of the owner of the drawings.

"I think they're really beautiful," the first voice repeated.

Sema felt the owner of the drawings smiling, even though she couldn't see his face.

"Thank you very much," said the owner.

The girl by the window took out her notebook. Sema saw the name on the cover.

"Toprak."

She put her blue pencil case back on the table and placed it next to the notebook. Then she opened the notebook's pages. Sema's eyes caught a painted human figure.

This human figure was wearing a maroon sweater.

Sema's blood rushed to her ears. She turned her attention to the outside.

She watched the passers-by for a while. After a few deep breaths, she remembered why she loved this cafe so much. She was angry with herself for sitting at a table that had never had access to this beautiful view until that day.

Without taking anything out of her bag and without ordering anything, she got up from her seat. She headed home. She was almost running.

As soon as she entered the house, she threw her bag and headed for the wardrobe. She took off the maroon sweater from the hanger for the first time.

She left the house again and headed back to the cafe without even taking her bag. When she looked inside on the way, she saw that the table where she had been sitting was still empty.

She walked in without looking at the girl by the window and sat at the table.

She was wearing the maroon sweater.

After taking a deep breath, she looked towards the other table by the window.

And she made eye contact with Toprak.
