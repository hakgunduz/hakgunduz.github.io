---
title: "Dream Seizures"
date: 2023-01-15 14:32:26 +0000
---

My grandmother told me one day that she could fly with two tree branches.

I thought she was joking and laughed. I was trying to get to my computer without forgetting what was on my mind. I rushed to my room. When I turned on the computer, I saw a draft of the email from the night before.

I couldn't sleep until four the night before. I had no desire to watch a TV series or read a book also. I just scrolled on the internet for hours. Finally, a design about space that I saw reminded me of my thesis, and with the impetus given by the feeling of guilt, I intended to send an e-mail to my teacher.

While I was thinking about what to write, I remembered that I couldn't put together the last part of my thesis very well and gave up on sending the e-mail. The desire in me disappeared as soon as it arrived. I closed the mail and started looking for the recipe my friend asked for. However, I couldn't remember the name of the dish.

When I went down to ask my grandmother, there was no one in the hall. I started searching inside the house and at that time I heard voices coming from the garden. My grandmother was trying to pluck a branch from the young olive tree in the garden.

“Grandma, what are you doing?” I approached her.

“Can't you see I'm trying to pick a nice branch? I will fly,” she said. She was trying to tear off the branch of the tree she could barely reach.

“Fly? Come on, let's go inside, you're going to fall and hurt yourself now, come on."

I grabbed her arm and escorted her home. As she walked, she flapped the single branch in her hand as if it were floating in the air.

She dropped the branch at the entrance of the house and her posture changed in an instant. When she looked at me before, it was as if she didn't see me. Now we met in the moment. She squeezed my arm and walked towards the kitchen. She began to collect the plates left over from the evening.

At that moment, I saw my computer on the table and remembered why I had come downstairs. “Grandma, what was the name of that eggplant dish you made?”

“Its name?” she was surprised. “It has no name, I made it up,” she said.

“A friend of mine asked for the recipe, anyway. Grandma, are you okay?"

“What shall we do tomorrow morning? Last you bought an avocado, where is it? Shall we try it? I don't know how to do it though," she said.

I didn't want to go too far, as I felt like she was avoiding my questions a bit. “We'll think about it tomorrow morning,” I said, helping her clean the kitchen.

Again, at the end of a sleepless night, when it was almost sunrise, I was looking at my thesis subject.

“Funding Processes of Space Research and Effects of Projects on Economy”.

I couldn't have chosen a dumber topic… I wondered if I should choose a neater topic and rewrite the whole thing as I fell asleep.

When I woke up in the morning, facing the thesis again made me startled. The day before, I was very upset that I could not share the recipe for the eggplant dish with my friend. So the first thing I did that morning was to start researching recipes.

After a not-so-lucky search, I went down to breakfast and saw newspaper clippings on the table. Ever since I told my grandmother that I was planning a career in writing about space exploration, she had been keeping all the news she saw about space.

Every time I see the news clippings, the thought comes to mind that I once wanted to be an investigative journalist writing about space. However, I could not even finish the thesis and submit it. My grandmother tried to convince me about this for a long time.

As she faced my indomitable laziness, she eventually stopped talking about it. Now she did nothing but collect occasional newspaper clippings. It had become a hobby for her.

There was also a small figurine next to the news clippings that day. It looked like a snow globe, but it was black, and the glowing things inside made you feel like you were looking into the depths of space. After looking at the figurine for a while in awe, I came to my senses with the sounds in the kitchen.

I felt a strange uneasiness and thought I could hardly bear the trinket being visible anywhere. I threw it in my drawer where I stacked the news clippings in my room.

As I was going downstairs again, I heard voices coming from the kitchen. There was no one in the house except my grandmother and me. When I entered the kitchen, my grandmother did not hear me come to the door. She was talking to the cups that she had in her hands while she was cleaning up the dishes.

“Serpil, look, you were fighting with Fulya the other day. If you do that, I won't put you in the same room together," she said to the two cups in her hand.

“Grandma,” I said, approaching her. She didn't give any reaction. “Come on, get some rest, I'll tidy this place up. Then let's go to the hospital together. You haven't gone for your check-up in a long time."

My grandmother's shoulders dropped. She left without saying anything and went to get ready. Fifteen minutes later we were on our way. It was as if my grandmother had shut herself off, neither speaking nor looking around. It was like someone had pulled the plug. She only moved when she needed to move.

The neurologist first started by asking a variety of questions. We talked about the health problems my grandmother had in the past, the diseases she continued to treat, and the medicine she used. My grandmother didn't react to anything I said. I was the only one talking to the doctor.

I described the symptoms of the last few days. I knew exactly when it started. After the symptoms, the doctor's questions were about my grandmother's memory. We talked about whether she had a focus problem. We evaluated her daily life behaviors such as speaking, reading, writing, and walking skills.

As we talked, I was relieved because my grandmother looked very good in general. Except for those two last moments, she was in her usual state. But the moment I avoided the most came immediately after this relief. The doctor asked if we had Alzheimer's disease in our family.

My grandmother's sister had Alzheimer's, and we lost her within a year. The doctor's demeanor suddenly became serious and he gave instructions with specific dates. He talked about the examination, tests, and radiological examinations. On the other hand, he was trying to convince me that we could diagnose very early and slow down the process as much as possible.

While all this was going on, what hurt me the most was that my grandmother was still not responding to anything. When we got home, she instantly went back to normal. She started to continue her housework from where she left off. She even came back to talk to me. She remembered the eggplant dish I asked for. She said she would write the recipe for me.

Hospitals became a part of our lives. The neurologist was having a hard time diagnosing. Even though the biggest doubt was Alzheimer's, there was something that didn't sit well with him.

When I went down to breakfast a few days later, the recipe for the eggplant dish was on the table, written on a piece of paper. My grandmother remembered the day I would meet my friend that day, to whom I would give the recipe. I felt peaceful for a second.

When I called my grandmother inside the house to say thank you, I saw that she was outside. When I saw what she was doing in the garden, I felt as if all the blood had been drained from my body.

“Grandma, what are you doing?” I could ask.

“Nilay, come and see! Snow is so beautiful. I'm making a snowman. Let's do it together!”

It didn't snow that day...

I had no idea what kept me from falling to the ground at that moment. I walked over to my grandma with tears in my eyes. "Come on, you'll be cold," I said. She came with me without any objection.

I almost gave up going out that night, but my grandma forced me out. “Share the eggplant recipe with your friends, get some air. It's good,” she said.

I met my friends at a bar in the city center. Before going out, I put the recipe written by my grandmother in a notebook. When I was chatting with my friends, the subject came to space as it always did. Eyes would turn to me, questions would be asked and answers would be expected.

Today's topic was a video Julide saw. She was beginning to have a fear of unidentified flying objects. She was waiting for a glimmer of relief from me. I tried to get away with giving evasive answers. Then the questions again evolved into the state of my thesis.

I knew that my friends did not ask me these questions to be mean. They were curious about what was going on in my life. So I changed the subject with my usual reproaches. I could talk endlessly about how intolerable Leo men are.

All of my friends knew very well who I was talking about when this topic was brought up. But they never mentioned his name. I have to give them credit for this. I have very sweet friends.

“Following your dreams and goals is of course very beautiful and important, but what's the point if you can't see anything on this road? They say you are the most important thing in their lives. If a Leo man says this, never believe it. The most important thing for them is always themselves.”

Everyone was nodding. Julide jumped in and said, “No one knows what they want, how do they know? They act as if they have all the answers. It's so annoying. ”

“Their wishes should always come first,” Zeynep supported. Fortunately, there were a few other people in the group who had suffered similar problems because of the Leo men. I was always able to use this to change the conversation.

At the end of the night, while saying goodbye in front of the bar door, I wanted to give Zeynep the recipe written by my grandmother. I couldn't find my notebook when I reached into my bag. After searching in a panic, I realized that I had dropped it somewhere. I hated myself.

I didn't know what to do. Julide had already sprinted inside to see if the notebook had fallen somewhere around the table, "it's not in there," she said.

I am a complete idiot. Why didn't I take a picture of the recipe? I apologized to Zeynep a thousand times. I was very afraid of letting down my friend. I couldn't even send a recipe.

I said goodbye to my friends and started walking home. After walking three or four hundred meters on the pavement, I had to cross the traffic lights. I was insulting myself along the way. I was sick to my stomach for being such an idiot. I was such an idiot.

When I crossed the traffic lights, I saw something on the ground a little ahead. As I got closer, I realized that the thing on the floor was my notebook. The recipe was still inside. I had the notebook in my hand. I just stared at the flashing red traffic light that was working for the empty streets.

The next day we had an appointment with the neurologist. When I woke up in the morning, I went downstairs to the sounds coming from the kitchen and the smell of cooking. My grandmother made enough food for an army.

“I have guests,” she said with great excitement.

“What guests? Who are they?"

“Serpil and Fulya live in silverware racks. We also called Sevgi, who lives in the cellar. Next time, our friends from the kitchen cupboards will come too.”

I was staring in shock.

“My best friend Feriye will also be in the best corner,” she said, pointing to the pressure cooker.

I reminded my grandmother of our doctor's appointment while making a note in my mind of handing out the meals to the neighbors. Her excitement faded in an instant. Her shoulders slumped, and she went to her room without even looking back at what was cooking on the stove.

I told the neurologist what had happened that morning. He viewed it positively that she had not lost his motor skills. A brain CT scan was taken that day. However, even after the results came, the diagnosis still could not be made. The image of my grandmother as she entered the tomography device broke my heart.

When we got home in the evening, I wanted to put my grandmother to rest immediately, but when I entered her room, all the laundry was scattered. I sat her in front of the TV and went to tidy her room.

The first thing I did was tidy up the socks on the floor. I didn't know where she put her socks, so I searched for their place in the closets. When I opened the first drawer of the nightstand, I found a lot of notepads, old photos, and pens. I've never seen these before.

Everything seemed to be arranged and stacked. In the middle of it was a trinket that looked like a small piece of paper. There was something unreadable written on it.

A notepad stood in the left corner with a pen attached to the corner as if it was used frequently. When I opened the first page, I saw the note “For the poetry competition”. There was also the note "1979" at the bottom.

I felt very guilty about tampering with my grandmother's personal belongings, but I couldn't help myself either. The notebook was not filled. I opened the last page where the notes ended. It started with the note "May 23".

The following text “3 months left” was underlined. Just below there was an attempt of a poem. It started with the sentence "I stopped flying out of my mind."

She was talking about tree branches floating in the wind, birds landing on them, and falling snow. At the same time, there were references to household items, drawing attention to the rhythm of daily life. Cups, plates, pots…

At the bottom was a sentence that was crossed out but still readable.

“A bird trapped in a house it built.”

I found an envelope in the notebook with an address for the poetry competition on it.

She did not apply for the competition…

From the first day I saw my grandmother's symptoms, a barrier seemed to have formed inside me, blocking my feelings that were trying to flow. What I read in that notebook broke the barrier inside me. I sat down and started crying.

…

When I woke up the next day, my grandmother told me about her adventures with the bird family. I listened with great enthusiasm and asked questions to learn more. Then we prepared a nice breakfast and ate together laughing.

Our meetings with the neurologist continued for weeks. It became a part of our lives. The doctor never seemed too worried.

A few days later, I left the poetry book I bought from the city center on the table without my grandmother seeing it. I never found out when she took from the table or whether she read it. But my grandmother's seizures subsided.

When I sent her a video I saw on the internet, she got very excited and offered to do the same in our city. She would organize meetings when housewives would gather and recite poems. I said I would help too. I would go door to door to find participants.

I submitted my thesis and graduated at about the same time. I got good feedback from my teacher and my friends at the university. Even my path to being a research assistant seemed to have opened up a bit.

One day, while I was having dinner with my grandmother, I told her that I had started a new article. I was writing an interesting and instructive article about space using people's obsession with seeing unidentified flying objects.

I've never seen my grandmother smile so beautifully before.
