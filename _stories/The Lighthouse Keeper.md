The light had not failed in forty years, and Mara intended to keep it that way.

Every evening she climbed the hundred and twelve steps, counted them out loud, and wiped the lens until the glass held her face like a second moon. The ships never thanked her. She did not expect them to.

---

On the night the storm came in from the west, the radio went quiet first. Then the generator coughed. Mara stood in the dark at the top of the tower, listening to the sea try every door at once.

> You keep a light for the people you will never meet.

Her father had said that. She had thought it was sentimental. Now, matches shaking in her hand, she understood it was simply instructions.

The wick caught.
