---
title: "Expeditions of Scaredy Tom, Part 1"
date: 2023-04-30 18:39:51 +0000
---

"Are they not asking for the purple orb?" the old man asked. Tom thought he would be alone when he came to this circle. However, the old man was sitting in a corner drinking something from his flask.

"No, they want the blue orb," Tom said. He wasn't very confident because the orbs' colors were similar.

He could distinguish the glowing colors of the orbs in the circle. Blue and purple hues were dominant. The circles were small plant settlements in the forest, where the roots of trees surrounded the orbs like a labyrinth.

In many circles, orbs were hidden by plants. Normally, finding orbs was a task that required great skill, and not everyone could do it. However, the circle Tom was sent to was simpler than the others.

In every other circle, there was one orb, while in this small circle, there were multiple orbs. However, only one of the orbs was real. It was also quite easy to choose from its color.

Although Tom was torn between the blue and purple orbs, he headed towards the blue one because he was afraid of his tribe's reaction.

"I think you're making a mistake," the old man said. "I'm sure they want the purple orb."

"They specifically instructed me to get the blue one," Tom replied hastily.

He had seen that adults were punished when their duties were not fulfilled in his tribe. Since Tom was only twelve years old, he had not been punished so far. However, they were always scolding him for his mistakes. He began to feel the pressure more and more as time passed.

He remembered the insults the tribal guardian had said to the others. He trembled as he gently plucked the blue orb from among the plants and put it in his straw bag.

He did not handle the plants the way he was taught while plucking the orbs. He should have pulled them out quickly and harshly. However, Tom felt guilty for treating the plants that way.

But he was also angry with himself for not behaving as expected.

He headed for the small town where they lived, ignoring the presence of the old man. The town was quite close. Children were not usually sent to distant circles. They were trained in nearby circles, and when they reached adulthood, they went on trips to collect orbs.

The children of the town couldn't see their families for years because of these trips. However, every tribe acted like a family. Children became the tribe's children until they saw their real parents. This ensured that they embraced their tribe rather than their small families when they grew up.

When he returned to the town, he saw the caretaker in the tower next to the entrance gate. A yellow glow rose from where the caretaker was, and the heavy, large entrance doors, which were quite heavy, began to open.

When they first made this gate, he thought that building such a large and heavy gate, which depended on the power of the orbs, was a bad idea. However, no one ever asked him for his opinion. That's why he didn't say anything.

Upon entering the town, Tom saw the villagers carrying their harvest with the help of patch brown orbs. The movement of the transport vehicles was facilitated by these orbs.

A smell reached his nose. When he turned his head, he saw red and orange smoke coming from the bakers’ tent. "Fresh bread day," he thought. He hoped that one of his tribe members had bought fresh bread.

The tribe members lived in large areas that could be called big tents. These big tents had sleeping areas in the form of small tents inside. While the tribes spent time in their own tents, the coming and going of other tribe members was also common.

Among all the tribes, there was only one tribe that did not have its own tent. This tribe's name was the Artificers. They had taught Tom to hide the orbs from them. The only thing he knew about their members was that they did not contribute anything to tribal societies. He had never seen any of them doing any necessary work.

Tom saw the caretaker by the fire in the middle of their large tent. He couldn't understand how quickly he had come from the gate. "Maybe he used the transport orb?" he said to himself.

When he arrived at the fire, he saw the tribal elders with the caretaker. They seemed to pay little attention to Tom's arrival.

He approached them hesitantly. He took the blue orb out of his bag and placed it in the corner of the fire. At that moment, his eyes were drawn to the little red-haired girl who was walking around the other fires. She was watching her surroundings with great interest. She was not from Tom's tribe.

The tribal elders looked at the orb that Tom had left by the fire. They had disappointed expressions on their faces. The caretaker, who was with them, must have understood this, as he immediately spoke up.

"We asked you for the blue orb, but you brought the wrong orb," said the caretaker. "This orb is light blue."

Tom wanted to answer, but he remembered that he had only angered the caretaker when he had tried to respond before. So, he thought they must have meant the purple orb.

Tom's eyes were drawn back to the little red-haired girl. Green sparkles were coming out of her hands. Green orbs were generally used in fields or forests. "Why is she using a green orb here?" he thought.

"Take the orb to the cooks," said the caretaker. The orbs that were of no use were taken to the cooks. Although they had no special properties, the orbs' glitter was sufficient to light a fire.

The little red-haired girl danced around the fires as if she were singing a song in her mind and accompanying it with the green sparkles coming out of her hands.

Tom put the orb back in his bag and headed towards the small fire areas. The cooks were there. He threw the light blue orb into the orb pile. At that moment, the little red-haired girl, who was watching him, had also noticed these orbs.

But Tom hadn't noticed this, because he could hear the conversations between the tribal elders and the caretaker.

"Incompetent," said one of the elders.

"I'm trying to train him, but he is just not enough," said the caretaker.

"We're wasting our time," said another of the elders.

One of the elders who had not spoken until then said, "I have an idea," and pulled the caretaker aside. He whispered something in his ear.

By then, Tom had finished his work and returned to the main fire. The caretaker bowed to the elders and left. Tom followed him. He was curious about what the red-haired little girl was doing, but they were walking in different directions now.

The little girl took out a gray orb from the pile of orbs. As she revealed the glow of the orb, it suddenly turned black. The black orb's glow was used for medical purposes. Tom wondered how the little girl turned the gray orb into black.

But he couldn't see what the girl was doing with the orb. He could not see any more of the tribal members around them now. They walked silently for a while.

"Tom, what kind of person do you want to be?" asked the caretaker.

Tom wasn't expecting this question. He thought for a while. He couldn't remember his father or mother. He couldn't aspire to be like them. He thought of people in the tribe he could look up to. He didn't want to be like the caretaker. Even though he always wanted to be someone who followed rules and was strict, he couldn't seem to do it.

"You want to have a job in our tribe," said the caretaker. Tom realized that the previous question wasn't really a question.

"Your job defines what kind of person you will be," continued the caretaker. "Look at our tribe. Everyone is involved in a job. We work together. My work makes it easier for others. Their work makes my life easier. But you need personal abilities to have a job."

Tom remembered the words he had overheard in the conversation:

"Incompetent, not enough..."

"That's why we're giving you a very important task," said the caretaker. "You will go and explore different circles."

Tom didn't know how to answer. He didn't understand what that meant.

"You'll take notes about these places so that when we go to these unknown circles later, we can find and extract the orbs more easily. This is a very important task," the caretaker continued.

He had never heard of a child being sent on a trip like this. The caretaker continued talking as if he understood what Tom was thinking.

"Don't confuse this task with trips. You will only go to nearby unknown circles. You have no responsibility to bring back the orbs. Your only job is to only bring back information," he said.

Tom nodded.

"Go run to the cooks and have them prepare stuff for your expeditions."

"Am I leaving now?" Tom asked.

"Yes, the faster you bring back information, the better it will be for our tribe," said the caretaker, handing Tom a map. He quickly introduced Tom to the unknown circles on the map.

“There are so many,” Tom thought.

"Don't disgrace our tribe,” the caretaker said. And left.
